# **The Cognitive Architecture of Incongruity Resolution: Information Processing, Neurological Reward, and Semantic Targeting**

The evolution of humor research has transitioned from early philosophical speculation to a rigorous branch of cognitive psychology and neuroscience, primarily driven by the Incongruity-Resolution (IR) framework established in the early 1970s. This framework posits that the experience of humor is not a random emotional outburst but a structured cognitive process involving the detection and subsequent resolution of logical discrepancies. The seminal work of Jerry Suls (1972) and Thomas R. Shultz (1976) provided the initial information-processing models that define this field, identifying a bi-phasic or tri-phasic sequence where the brain encounters an expectation-defying stimulus and seeks a cognitive rule to reconcile it.1 This mechanism serves as a neurological engine, where the resolution of a conceptual problem triggers a dopaminergic reward, effectively making laughter the physiological byproduct of successful cognitive labor.5

## **The Genesis of Incongruity-Resolution Theory**

The conceptual foundations of IR theory are rooted in the shift from the Relief and Superiority theories of the early 20th century toward an Information-Processing paradigm. While historical figures like Kant described humor as the sudden transformation of a strained expectation into nothing, and Schopenhauer viewed it as the incongruity between a concept and a real object, it was not until Suls (1972) that these observations were codified into a quantifiable cognitive model.4 Suls proposed a two-stage model that treats humor as a problem-solving task. In stage one, the recipient processes a setup that establishes a structure of expectations; the arrival of the punchline violates these expectations, creating a state of cognitive dissonance or incongruity.3 In stage two, the recipient engages in a search for a cognitive rule or a logical bridge that allows the punchline to follow naturally from the setup, albeit through a previously unconsidered perspective.4

This model moved humor research into the realm of experimental psychology, allowing for the measurement of response latencies and cognitive load. The humor response is thus defined as the specific affective reaction to the successful mastery of this incongruity. If the incongruity is too small, the stimulus is perceived as mundane; if the incongruity is too large and cannot be resolved, it is perceived as nonsense or confusion.12 The following table summarizes the primary distinctions between the foundational models of this era and their core mechanics.

| Model / theorist | Core Mechanism | Focus | Key Contribution |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Suls (1972)** | Two-Stage (Incongruity \-\> Resolution) | Information Processing | Codified humor as a problem-solving event with specific timing. |
| **Shultz (1976)** | Cognitive-Developmental Analysis | Child Development | Distinguished between resolvable incongruity and pure nonsense. |
| **Rothbart (1976)** | Arousal-Safety Mechanism | Affective/Cognitive | Linked the resolution of incongruity to a decrease in psychological tension. |
| **Sperber & Wilson (1995)** | Relevance Theory | Pragmatics | Humor as a "mutual parallel adjustment" of context and inference. |
| **Raskin (1985)** | Semantic Script Theory (SSTH) | Linguistics | Focused on the overlap of incompatible semantic scripts. |

### **The Sulsian Information-Processing Model**

Suls’ 1972 model characterizes the joke as a linear text that the perceiver attempts to predict as they consume it. The setup provides the data necessary to build a mental script or schema. When the punchline arrives, it acts as a disruption condition.4 The cognitive agent does not simply abandon the original schema; instead, they perform a backward chaining process to locate the precise point where the initial interpretation diverged from the true meaning hidden in the setup.9 This process is inherently non-monotonic, meaning that previously held conclusions are withdrawn in favor of a new, more comprehensive interpretation that accounts for the punchline.9

The efficiency of this stage is paramount. If the resolution requires too many inferential steps or too much retrieval from the storehouse of information, the humor response is attenuated. This suggests that the brain prefers optimal innovation—a shift that is surprising but easily accessible once the key is provided.9 The resolution makes the punchline follow naturally from the setup, effectively transforming an illogical situation into a logical one within the specialized universe of the joke.4

### **Shultz and the Resolution vs. Nonsense Dichotomy**

Thomas R. Shultz extended the Sulsian model by investigating the developmental trajectory of humor in children and the structural differences between complete and partial resolution. Shultz identified that young children may find simple incongruity (e.g., a man wearing a fish as a hat) funny even without a resolution, whereas older children and adults require a reason for the incongruity to fully appreciate the wit.3 He defined resolution as the subtle aspect of jokes which renders incongruity meaningful or appropriate by explaining it.3

One of Shultz's most profound insights was the distinction between resolution humor and nonsense humor. In resolution humor, the incongruity is fully accounted for by the punchline (e.g., a pun where a word's second meaning fits perfectly). In nonsense humor, the resolution is only partial; it explains the joke but leaves a residue of logical impossibility or absurdity.12 This residue prevents the brain from closing the file on the problem, leading to what some researchers call a recursive reward where the brain keeps trying to fully resolve the absurdity and receives a pulse of satisfaction each time it achieves a partial fit.12

## **Neurological Mechanics: The Reward of Resolution**

The experience of humor is a high-order cognitive event involving an extensive network of brain regions. Neuroimaging studies, primarily using fMRI and ERP (Event-Related Potentials), have mapped the temporal and spatial dynamics of the humor response, revealing that the "aha" moment of resolution is inextricably linked to the brain's mesolimbic reward system.6

### **The Dopaminergic Reward Circuit**

When a listener "gets" a joke, the brain shifts from a state of information processing to an emotional response generated in the nucleus accumbens and the ventral tegmental area (VTA).5 This is the same reward circuit that reinforces survival-essential behaviors such as eating and social bonding. The release of dopamine in these centers functions as a neurological payoff for the cognitive work of solving the incongruity. This reward serves an evolutionary purpose: it incentivizes the brain to engage in flexible thinking and pattern recognition, which are critical for navigating complex environments.5

The prefrontal cortex (PFC) acts as the executive controller of this process. It oversees the detection of the "twist," performs a relevance check, and, if the information is deemed attention-worthy, directs the signal toward the reward centers.5 The medial ventral prefrontal cortex, in particular, has been identified as a universal humor region that activates regardless of whether the joke is a pun (phonetic) or a semantic narrative.6

### **Temporal Dynamics and ERP Signatures**

Electrophysiological studies have provided a millisecond-by-millisecond account of humor comprehension. The process follows a distinct temporal pattern:

1. **500–800 ms (N400/N500-800):** Detection of the incongruity. Humorous and unrelated stimuli both elicit a negative ERP deflection, reflecting the brain's realization that the incoming information does not fit the current schema.2  
2. **800–1000 ms (P800-1000):** Classification and association evaluation. The brain evaluates whether there are attainable cues to form an association between the context and the surprising element.2  
3. **1000–1600 ms:** Incongruity resolution. A positive slow wave emerges, involved in the formation of novel associations and the switching of perspectives.2  
4. **1600–2000 ms:** Affective response. A positive deflection (P1600-2000) corresponds to the emotional processing and subjective feeling of amusement.2

This timeline proves that humor is a slow cognitive process compared to basic linguistic processing, as it requires re-analysis and perspective-shifting. The delay between the punchline and the laugh is the window where the brain is performing the backward chaining necessary for resolution.9

| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Temporo-Parietal Junction (TPJ)** | Integration of multi-sensory info; Perspective taking | Resolution |
| **Inferior Frontal Gyrus (IFG)** | Processing incongruity; Semantic retrieval | Detection / Resolution |
| **Nucleus Accumbens** | Dopamine release; Reward assessment | Affective Response |

## **Information Architecture: The Requirement of Brevity**

In written and verbal humor, the structure of the information is the primary determinant of its efficacy. The Sulsian model suggests that for a joke to be architecturally sound, it must adhere to strict parameters regarding setup length and punchline economy. This is often described as the Humor and Duration Principle: the less time taken to reach the resolution, the stronger the humor response.23

### **Word Economy as a Neurological Mandate**

The concept of word economy in humor is not merely an aesthetic preference; it is a neurological requirement. Every extra word in a punchline increases the cognitive load and dilutes the speed of the resolution reward.23 Professional comedy writers spend a significant amount of time stripping away unnecessary verbiage to ensure the punch is delivered as a single, clean conceptual unit.

Consider the Mitch Hedberg joke: *"I'm against picketing, but I don't know how to show it"*.23 The brilliance of this joke lies in its shrinkage. A novice writer might fluff the punchline: *"I'm against picketing, but I don't know if I should protest it with a sign or whatever."* This version fails because the resolution—the irony of using a sign to protest signs—is buried under extra words, allowing the audience too much time to predict the ending or losing the shock of the logic switch.23

### **Setup-Incongruity-Resolution (SIR) Timing**

The SIR timing requires that the setup contains exactly enough information to prime the schema but not enough to give away the resolution. If the setup is too long, the audience has more time to engage in expectancy generation, which may lead them to guess the punchline, thereby eliminating the surprise component.21 If the setup is too short, the incongruity may not be registered as a violation of a robust schema, leading to a weak resolution.9

## **Semantic Affinity and the Primed Cognitive Field**

A rising conceptual domain in an audience's collective attention creates what is known as a primed cognitive field. In this state, the brain is already pattern-matching around a specific set of schemas. This has profound implications for humor targeting—the strategic use of semantic affinity data, extracted from sources like Instagram and YouTube comments, to select the highest-probability targets for incongruity resolution.27

### **The Mechanism of Semantic Priming**

Semantic priming is the psychological phenomenon where exposure to one stimulus (the prime) influences the response to a subsequent stimulus (the target).26 In humor, the prime is the conceptual domain the audience is already engaged with. If a comedian or advertiser uses a joke that draws on a domain currently in the audience's working memory, the resolution pathway is already pre-activated.29 This reduces the effortful processing required to resolve the incongruity, leading to a faster and more intense reward.

Studies on lexical decision tasks show that reaction times are significantly decreased when words are preceded by semantically related concepts.26 This processing head start means that the brain can resolve the incongruity of a joke more quickly if the logic key belongs to a semantic domain the audience has recently accessed.

## **Absurdism: The Power of Partial Resolution**

While Suls focused on the complete resolution of jokes, Shultz and later researchers identified that absurdist or nonsense humor often produces a stronger and more sustained humor response. This is structurally explained by the resolvability of the incongruity.

### **Resolvable vs. Irresolvable Incongruity**

In Incongruity-Resolution Humor (IRH), the punchline provides a clear, logical rule that reconciles the setup. In Nonsense Humor (NH), the resolution is only playful or partial. The brain detects a logic rule, but that rule itself is absurd or violates reality.12 Neuroimaging reveals that NH elicits greater activation in the fusiform gyrus and the superior temporal gyrus, areas associated with word processing and pragmatic awareness, as the brain continues to search for a more complete meaning.12

This absurdist residue creates a recursive loop. Because the brain is a natural problem-solving machine, it cannot easily discard a partially resolved incongruity. Each time the brain re-examines the joke, it re-resolves the playful rule and receives another pulse of the reward signal. This explains why absurdist humor is more re-watchable than straightforward puns; the brain keeps getting the resolution reward each time it tries to close the logical loop.12

### **Absurdity in Advertising and Persuasion**

The use of absurdity and humor in advertising is a strategic tool to break resistance to influence. Research by Strick et al. (2012) demonstrates that humor processing consumes significant limited cognitive resources.17 Because the brain is occupied with the cognitive-linguistic task of resolving the incongruity, it has fewer resources available to generate counter-arguments or negative brand associations.15

1. **Distraction Component:** The IR process is a high-load task that distracts the viewer from the persuasive intent of the ad.17  
2. **Positive Affect Component:** The dopamine release from the resolution creates a halo effect or affect transfer, where the positive feelings from the joke are associated with the brand.17

This dual mechanism explains why humorous ads are often more effective even when they contain weak logical arguments for the product itself; the humor bypasses the critical processing faculty of the audience.17

## **Humor as a Problem-Solving Analogy**

A profound third-order insight from the research is the structural similarity between getting a joke and scientific discovery. Both processes rely on the resolution of an incongruity. In science, an unexpected experimental discovery or anomaly violates the prevailing theory (the setup).9 The scientist must then find a new theory (the resolution) that makes the anomaly seem expected.

The reasoning in both humor and science is non-monotonic and involves:

* **Backward Chaining:** Pinpointing the element in previous knowledge that led to the mistaken expectation.9  
* **Forward Chaining:** Reasoning toward a new model or interpretation that integrates the surprising data.9

The following table compares the structural stages of humor comprehension with those of scientific discovery and thought experimentation as described in cognitive linguistics.

| Stage | Humor Comprehension | Scientific Discovery | Thought Experimentation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Initial State** | Setup / Primed Schema | Prevailing Theory / Paradigm | Conceptual Framework |
| **Incongruity** | Punchline / Logic Violation | Anomalous Data / Observation | Logical Paradox / Conflict |
| **Cognitive Search** | Search for "Cognitive Rule" | Hypothesis Generation | Re-analysis of Premises |
| **Resolution** | Reframed Logical Meaning | New Theoretical Model | Conceptual Shift / New Schema |
| **Reward** | Laughter / Amusement | "Eureka" Moment / Insight | Clarity / Logical Consistency |

This suggests that humor is not just entertainment but a playful exercise for the brain's most advanced problem-solving faculties. It trains the mind to be comfortable with cognitive shifts and to seek creative resolutions to logical conflicts.9

## **Strategic Synthesis for the Humor Skill Guide**

For the development of a professional Humor Skill Guide or an automated content generation system, the research identifies several encodable parameters. These are not aesthetic judgments but structural requirements dictated by the brain's information-processing architecture.4

### **1\. Incongruity Placement and Depth**

The Incongruity Gap must be calibrated based on the audience's cognitive capacity. If the gap is too narrow, the resolution is trivial and the reward is low. If it is too wide, the audience may fail to find the resolution, leading to nonsense-aversiveness.12 The depth of the incongruity can be increased if the domain is pre-primed through semantic affinity targeting.

### **2\. The Economy of the Punchline**

The punchline must be the shortest possible phrase that contains the logic key. Every extra syllable increases the time-to-resolution (![][image12]), which inversely correlates with humor intensity (![][image3]). Content creators should prioritize high-impact shrinkage during the editing phase.23

### **3\. The Absurdist Decision Tree**

Creators must decide whether to use Complete Resolution (for satisfaction and clarity) or Partial Resolution/Absurdism (for sustained impact and re-watchability). Absurdism is particularly effective for high-engagement content where the goal is to have the audience return to the material multiple times.12

### **4\. Semantic Field Priming**

Humor targeting should begin with an analysis of Semantic Affinity Data. By extracting the conceptual domains most active in the target audience's digital discourse (e.g., trending topics on Instagram), creators can build setups that leverage pre-primed resolution pathways. This lowers the cognitive cost of the joke and increases the strength of the humor response.27

### **5\. Affective Regulation**

Humor serves a biosocial function of indicating playfulness. The resolution stage must signal that the violation is benign or non-threatening. If the incongruity remains perceived as a genuine threat or insult (without a clear playfulness signal), the brain stays in a state of arousal/distress rather than transitioning to the reward/amusement phase.41

## **The Role of Context and Sensation Seeking**

The appreciation of incongruity-resolution humor is not uniform across all populations. Personality variables, such as sensation seeking and tolerance for ambiguity, play a moderating role.13 High sensation seekers, characterized by a lower base level of arousal, tend to enjoy the arousal induction phase (the incongruity) more than those with high base arousal. For individuals with high base arousal, the resolution stage is particularly rewarding because it reduces the induced tension to a more comfortable level.38

Furthermore, the context of the humor presentation matters. Exposure to humorous material prior to a stressful event, such as a difficult test, has been shown to reduce state anxiety and enhance cognitive performance.44 This preventive function suggests that humor can be used strategically to regulate emotional states and facilitate better problem-solving in high-pressure environments.14

## **Linguistic and Social Mechanisms in IR Theory**

The Incongruity-Resolution theory is largely modality-independent, applying to verbal jokes, visual cartoons, and even silent humorous videos.46 However, the specific linguistic strategies used—such as reference assignment, disambiguation, and concept adjustment—determine the complexity of the resolution.4 For instance, "garden-path" jokes exploit the hearer's construction of an appropriate mental situation that eventually turns out to be incorrect, forcing a forced reinterpretation.4

Socially, humor serves as an interpersonal integration tool. By sharing a joke that relies on a specific set of shared schemas, individuals reinforce their membership in a social group. Conversely, failing to "get" a joke can signal social exclusion or a lack of shared cultural knowledge.42 The use of humor as an ideological struggle or a weapon against social norms further illustrates its power in shaping social reality and challenging established conventions.49

## **Conclusion: The Automated Future of Humor Targeting**

The Incongruity-Resolution Theory, as established by Suls and Shultz, remains the most robust framework for understanding the mechanics of humor. By identifying humor as a bi-phasic information-processing event—rather than a vague emotional state—these researchers provided the tools to quantify, encode, and target wit.

The convergence of this theory with modern neurological data and digital semantic mapping reveals that humor is a "killer app" of human cognition.51 It showcases the brain's ability to hold multiple incompatible frames of reference simultaneously (bisociation) and to find creative keys to resolve them. As we move toward more sophisticated AI-driven content creation, the parameters identified in these studies—setup length, incongruity placement, resolution economy, and semantic priming—will serve as the foundational code for generating human-resonant humor.

In summary, the neurological reward associated with laughter is a byproduct of successful problem-solving. By utilizing semantic affinity data to identify pre-primed cognitive domains, creators can engineer incongruities that are easier for the brain to resolve and more likely to trigger a powerful dopaminergic response. This transformation of humor from an "aesthetic judgment" to an "encodable parameter" represents the next frontier in cognitive targeting and communication strategy.1
