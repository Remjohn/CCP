### **F1 — The Change Talk Vault**

**Autonomous Commitment Reactivation Engine**

Grounding: Miller & Rollnick Motivational Interviewing (2012) \+ Amrhein et al. Change Talk Research (2003)

The persuader who speaks least wins most. Amrhein demonstrated that client-generated commitment language is a stronger predictor of follow-through than any coach argument. The causality is inverted from conventional sales logic: it is not what you say to someone that changes behavior, it is what you get them to say to themselves.

The Change Talk Vault is a dedicated Supabase table tagging and archiving every commitment-language statement a client generates across their entire CBCS history. Each statement is tagged with its DARN-CAT dimension (Desire, Ability, Reasons, Need, Commitment, Activation, Taking Steps), LIWC-22 emotional intensity score, and coping trajectory position at time of statement. When the Campaign Orchestration Agent prepares a conversion sequence, it queries the Vault for the client's highest-intensity, most-recent commitment statements. The invitation mirrors these back — the client's own commitment, now externally visible, enters a memory reconsolidation labile state where it can be reinforced and acted upon.

Market Comparison: every CRM sends re-engagement messages that begin with the company's message. The Change Talk Vault sends messages that begin with the client's own words. No conventional platform has access to this data because no platform has built what CBCS has built across 90 days of authentic journaling.

### **F2 — The Social Penetration Depth Gauge**

**Relationship Stage-Aware Campaign Logic with Dual-Condition Mood Gate**

Grounding: Altman & Taylor SPT (1973) \+ Collins & Miller Meta-Analysis (1994)

Collins and Miller's most critical finding: people like those to whom they have disclosed. The client who has shared L3 fears in CBCS journaling likes the coaching relationship more because they invested their inner self into it. The Social Penetration Depth Gauge tracks each client's position across the four SPT stages — computed from LIWC-22 authenticity scores and disclosure depth classification of every CBCS interaction.

V3.0 critical addition: the Gauge is combined with mood state from DEP-ENG-018 into a triple-condition delivery permission gate. An invitation fires only when ALL THREE conditions are simultaneously true: Relationship Stage ≥ Affective Exchange AND Mood State ≠ negative high-arousal AND Coping Trajectory \= SEARCH. Most CRMs are socially awkward precisely because they track behavioral proxies rather than the actual quality of the psychological relationship. The Gauge measures the relationship itself.

### **F3 — The Personal Relevance Trigger (Identity-First Architecture)**

**ELM Central Route Activation — Identity Not Behavior**

Grounding: Petty & Cacioppo ELM (1986) \+ Kreuter & Strecher Health Communication Tailoring (1996)

V3.0 critical refinement: the Personal Relevance Trigger is NOT behavioral pattern recognition. Telling someone 'you have restarted this goal four times' describes what they did, not who they are — this is peripheral processing. The genuine central route trigger is identity-first. The brain cannot dismiss an accurate identity statement without high-elaboration evaluation because identity is the operating system all decisions run through.

Personal relevance is what exists inside the client's current context window. Their identity is already loaded there — their unique reasons for why this matters specifically to them, their specific cognitive protection patterns, their particular emotional architecture. A message that speaks directly to what is live in their psychological context window requires zero translation to register as maximally relevant. It is already in the room.

Correct example: 'You have always known what you needed to do. The problem has never been information. The problem is that every time you get close enough to see it clearly, something in you decides it is safer to stay confused.' This demands central route elaboration because it describes the architecture of who they are — not what they have done.

### **F4 — The Information Coping Trajectory Mapper**

**Five-Position Content and Campaign Routing at Tribe AND Individual Level**

Grounding: Information Coping Trajectory (Positions 1-5) \+ Kreuter & Strecher (1996)

Five positions describe not just where a client is emotionally but what kind of information they can actually receive: Position 1 (Deficiency — shock), Position 2 (Ill-Informed — fear, denial), Position 3 (Needs Injection — active seeking, peak receptivity), Position 4 (Information Health — confident, deciding), Position 5 (Information Donor — mastery, altruistic). A Position 2 client who receives a high-complexity offer experiences increased fear and defensive reactions. The same offer to a Position 3 client lands as the answer to an active search.

V3.0 critical insight — operates at TWO levels simultaneously. Individual level: gates commercial invitations and determines offer tier appropriateness. Tribe level: governs the entire weekly CCF content production strategy. A tribe collectively in Position 2 needs content that validates and connects — not content that informs. The content archetype, emotional register, and CRAL research strategy for a full production week calibrate to the tribe's aggregate position.

This also resolves the community subscription homogeneity problem: the coaching industry almost universally treats clients within a program as a homogeneous group receiving the same curriculum at the same pace. A Position 3 and Position 5 client in the same community space are not having the same experience and cannot. The CBCS Trajectory Mapper individualizes content delivery through the same channel based on each client's individual position.

### **F5 — The 72-Hour Identity Anchor Protocol**

**Scientifically Spaced Pre-Invitation Micro-Commitment Architecture**

Grounding: Freedman & Fraser FITD (1966) \+ Cialdini Commitment and Consistency (1984) \+ Burger & Cornelius (2003)

Micro-commitments spaced across multiple interactions over days produce stronger identity anchoring than equivalent commitments in a single session. The brain interprets repeated evidence across time as a stable trait — 'I am someone who engages with this.' Three interactions over 72 hours, each leaving specific psychological residue without revealing the incoming invitation, transforms a neutral recipient into someone who has anchored their identity around exactly what the invitation represents.

Day Minus 3 — Observation Prompt: genuine cultural tension question from the Scheduled Monitor Agent. Client articulates their position on the exact tension the offer addresses. Identity Anchor: I care about this. Day Minus 2 — Competence Mirror: specific acknowledgment from CBCS history demonstrating the client already acts on this. Identity Anchor: I take this seriously. Day Minus 1 — Anticipation Signal: coach shares genuine excitement about something being prepared, zero offer details. Identity Anchor: something worth waiting for is coming. Day Zero: the invitation arrives to a client who has anchored their identity three times around exactly what it represents.

Critical rule: none of the three priming interactions can reference the offer, hint at a sale, or feel preparatory. If the client consciously recognizes they are being warmed up, the identity anchoring mechanism collapses and is replaced by reactance — psychological resistance that is the exact opposite of the intended effect.

### **F6 — The SEARCH Phase Detection Engine**

**LIWC-22 Powered Intervention Timing with Reconsolidation Window**

Grounding: Zeelenberg & Pieters Regret Regulation Theory (2007) \+ LIWC-22 \+ Nader Memory Reconsolidation (2000)

The most powerful motivational trigger following negative prior outcomes (dormancy) is the anticipation of regret from continued inaction. The SEARCH Phase Detection Engine monitors CBCS linguistic data for the specific cluster signaling peak intervention receptivity: drop in analytical thinking \+ spike in discrepancy words (would, could, should, want) \+ increased future-focus language \+ elevated self-referential pronouns \+ high cognitive processing indicators.

When this cluster crosses threshold, a 24-hour observation window confirms the signal is sustained rather than transient, then triggers Day Minus 3 of the 72-Hour Identity Anchor Protocol. The campaign at Day Zero requires no manufactured scarcity — the client is already experiencing genuine urgency from their own cognitive process. Timing the intervention to the reconsolidation window is the equivalent of surgery when the patient is physiologically ready rather than on an arbitrary schedule. The same message at the wrong moment lands on a consolidated, defended belief system.

### **F8 — The Transportation Score Gate**

**Voice Message Neurological Quality Assurance**

Grounding: Green & Brock Transportation-Imagery Model (2000) \+ Green Narrative Persuasion (2006)

The auditory channel is neurologically optimized for the deepest transportation states. Audio requires active co-creation of imagery from partial cues — the listener's own mental pictures, drawn from their experiential archive, are more emotionally resonant than any externally provided visual. Transported recipients change attitudes more readily, retain changes longer, and report higher confidence in decisions made during or after transportation.

Every voice message script must pass the Transportation Score Gate before delivery to the coach for recording. Four conditions must all be met: (1) Sensory Detail Presence — at least one concrete, specific, verifiable sensory detail triggering active imagery. (2) Distancing Language Absence — zero hedging qualifiers (I think, I believe, in my experience) that break narrative immediacy. (3) Prosodic Pattern Match — sentence rhythm, length distribution, and discourse marker placement match the coach's Voice DNA prosodic signature. (4) Narrative Arc Completeness — the script establishes a situation, moves through it, and arrives somewhere without naming the destination explicitly.

Countdown timers produce cortisol-driven defensive decision-making and systematic distrust when the deadline resets. Genuine temporal accuracy produces dopamine-driven approach motivation. Clients converting from genuine anticipated regret become committed buyers. Clients converting from manufactured urgency become immediate buyer's remorse.

# **The Coach Gradual Disclosure Protocol**

## **Reciprocal Intimacy Architecture — Critical V3.0 Addition**

Social Penetration Theory establishes that reciprocal disclosure is the mechanism that deepens relationships in both directions. A relationship where one party always discloses and the other always reflects is not mutual penetration — it is one-directional extraction. The client eventually feels this asymmetry and it produces the uncanny valley effect: intimate on the surface but hollow at the core.

### **Vulnerability Level Classification**

The Coach Story Archive (DEP-ENG-024) is reclassified in V3.0 as both a content production resource AND a disclosure library. Every entry receives a vulnerability level classification:

* L1 — Biographical fact, professional history, coaching approach, expertise areas

* L2 — Personal struggle, past failure, specific fear, private doubt, professional pivot moments

* L3 — Formative wound, deepest transformation moment, core driver of coaching work, current real-time challenge

### **Disclosure Depth Matching Rule**

The CBCS interaction engine governs coach disclosure to mirror the client's current SPT stage. Orientation → L1 coach disclosure. Exploratory Affective → L2 disclosure. Affective Exchange and Stable Exchange → L3 disclosure. The system never discloses deeper than one level above the client's current SPT stage — premature vulnerability creates the 'breaking norms' effect that Collins and Miller identify as decreasing liking rather than increasing it.

### **What Counts as Authentic Coach Disclosure**

A witnessed experience counts as disclosure when the coach's internal response is the primary content, not the observation itself. A coach sharing that a close friend went through a painful divorce is L1 biographical information. A coach sharing that watching that friend's divorce was the moment they realized they had been running the same self-protective pattern in their own life — that is L2 disclosure. The emotional truth of the witness experience is the disclosure.

* Personal transformation moments — formative experiences creating the coaching philosophy (L2/L3)

* Witnessed client transformations — client results stories where coach's emotional investment is expressed (L2)

* Personal life context — family dynamics, close relationships — when the coach's vulnerability in relation to these is the content (L2/L3)

* Professional failures and pivots — moments where the coaching approach was wrong (L2)

* Current real-time struggles — used sparingly, only in Stable Exchange contexts (L3 maximum)

### **Monthly Coach Interview Protocol**

The Coach Story Archive is refreshed monthly through a structured Telegram interview — 8-10 questions, 20 minutes maximum — conducted by the Coach Monthly Interview Agent (Guardian sub-agent). Questions target not just what the coach experienced but specifically how they felt about it, what it revealed about themselves, and what they would tell someone currently living through that same experience. Output: 5-8 new disclosure entries per month at various vulnerability levels. Over 12 months: 60-96 authentic entries the system deploys intelligently across hundreds of client interactions, always at the appropriate depth and moment.
