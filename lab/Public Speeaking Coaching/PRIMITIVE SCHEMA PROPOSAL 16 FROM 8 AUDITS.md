# Primitive Schema Proposal from 8 Audits

## Why this pass is different

This is not a "best ideas from the library" list.
It is a **resonance-biased primitive pass** built from 8 existing audits with one filter:

**keep the primitives that most directly help us create connection, surprise, and felt emotional movement.**

That means I am not treating all primitives as equal units.
Some are relational.
Some are narrative.
Some are performance-based.
Some are perceptual.
Some are sonic.

So the right schema cannot be flat.
It has to treat primitives more like **cells with different functions** inside one larger organism.

## The 16 primitives worth carrying forward

I intentionally chose **2 from each audit** so the set stays broad enough to be useful across CCF, CMF, CBCS, CVE, Telegram, and V2WS.

### 1. Irony Inversion
**Source:** `Audit_The_Elements_of_Humor_CCP.md`

This primitive creates surprise by exposing the gap between what people say they believe and what actually happens in life. It is a high-value resonance primitive because recognition plus contradiction produces both laughter and insight. This is strong for hooks, reframes, observational content, and anti-centroid writing.

### 2. Tribal Reference
**Source:** `Audit_The_Elements_of_Humor_CCP.md`

This primitive makes people feel "this is about us." It uses in-group language, lived cues, and shared symbolic references to create instant belonging. It is one of the fastest routes to connection because the audience does not merely understand the content; they feel socially located inside it.

### 3. Repetition Game / Live Contact
**Source:** `AUDIT_Sanford_Meisner_on_Acting.md`

This is not repetition as a rhetorical gimmick. It is contact. It forces the communicator to react to what is actually there instead of delivering pre-baked performance. For CCP, this is vital because it fights genericity and keeps delivery responsive, not canned.

### 4. Pinch and Ouch
**Source:** `AUDIT_Sanford_Meisner_on_Acting.md`

This primitive says behavior must be caused. Someone says something, something lands, then a response emerges. It is powerful for emotional credibility because it restores causal life to scripts, voice notes, and roleplays. Instead of ideas floating in space, something actually hits and creates an effect.

### 5. Superobjective
**Source:** `AUDIT_Steal_the_Show_Michael_Port.md`

This is the higher-order intention under the content. Not "what am I saying?" but "what is my deeper objective with this audience?" It improves resonance because content feels more coherent when every beat serves one human outcome: calm them, wake them up, dignify them, move them, challenge them.

### 6. Backstory Architecture
**Source:** `AUDIT_Steal_the_Show_Michael_Port.md`

Backstory gives emotional and behavioral legitimacy to delivery. It answers why this message matters, what wound or conviction powers it, and what hidden context gives the performance texture. This is one of the clearest anti-slop primitives because flat outputs usually lack lived pressure behind the words.

### 7. What Is / What Could Be
**Source:** `AUDIT_Resonate_Nancy_Duarte.md`

This is one of the best tension primitives in the whole library. It generates movement by oscillating between current reality and possible transformation. It is persuasive because it does not merely describe a better future; it makes the gap emotionally active.

### 8. S.T.A.R. Moment
**Source:** `AUDIT_Resonate_Nancy_Duarte.md`

This is the designed peak: something memorable, dramatic, visual, embodied, or emotionally charged that reorganizes attention and memory. It matters because people do not remember the average of an experience. They remember the peak.

### 9. Throughline
**Source:** `AUDIT_TED_Talks_Chris_Anderson.md`

This primitive creates coherence. It keeps the content from diffusing into topic soup. On its own it does not guarantee emotional power, but without it, resonance decays because the audience cannot carry the thing away in one mental unit.

### 10. Connection Before Content
**Source:** `AUDIT_TED_Talks_Chris_Anderson.md`

This is one of the purest connection primitives in the stack. It says permission, warmth, intimacy, and trust come first. Before teaching, persuading, or performing, we need to open a human channel.

### 11. Matching Principle
**Source:** `AUDIT_Supercommunicators.md`

This is the best real-time conversation primitive in the sample. It detects whether the other person is in a practical, emotional, or identity-layer conversation and matches the layer before redirecting. It is essential for Telegram, roleplay, coaching, and dynamic agentic responses.

### 12. Looping for Understanding
**Source:** `AUDIT_Supercommunicators.md`

This is proof-of-listening, not just listening. It deepens connection because the other person sees their meaning reflected back and verified. In human terms, it creates the feeling of being held in attention rather than processed.

### 13. Trojan Horse Narrative
**Source:** `AUDIT_Born_a_Crime.md`

This primitive wraps insight inside story, humor, or vivid human material so the lesson enters without resistance. It is one of the strongest surprise-plus-feeling primitives because the audience receives meaning before they erect defensive analysis around it.

### 14. Empathy Inversion
**Source:** `AUDIT_Born_a_Crime.md`

This primitive changes the angle of seeing. It takes a person, system, or situation the audience thinks they already understand and rotates the camera until hidden humanity or hidden structure becomes visible. This is one of the most transformational primitives because it enlarges moral imagination.

### 15. Sound as Attention Architecture
**Source:** `AUDIT_Designing_Sound_Beck_Kalinak.md`

This primitive says emotional communication is not just what is present, but what is given priority. For CMF and CVE it matters because sonic hierarchy controls felt meaning, emphasis, and pressure. It is a key primitive for "make them feel something" because sound can decide what the nervous system treats as important.

### 16. Auditory Point of View
**Source:** `AUDIT_Designing_Sound_Beck_Kalinak.md`

This primitive determines from whose inner or outer position the moment is heard. It is emotionally powerful because it changes intimacy. Public distance, private interiority, exposure, memory, pressure, and relief can all be signaled through auditory perspective.

## What these 16 suggest about the right ontology

The biggest lesson is that a primitive should not be stored as:

- a name
- a definition
- a tip

That is too weak.

If our real goal is to create content that **resonates, surprises, and makes people feel**, then a primitive should be modeled as:

**a typed operator that changes human state under certain conditions**

In other words:

`primitive(context_state) -> expected_delta_in_attention_trust_emotion_memory_action`

That is much closer to how the system should reason.

## Proposed top-level family structure

These 16 are not one family.
They naturally cluster into 6 families:

### A. Connection Primitives
- Tribal Reference
- Connection Before Content
- Matching Principle
- Looping for Understanding

These increase trust, belonging, social safety, and felt recognition.

### B. Surprise Primitives
- Irony Inversion
- What Is / What Could Be
- Trojan Horse Narrative
- Empathy Inversion

These create pattern break, reframe, tension, and cognitive opening.

### C. Emotional Credibility Primitives
- Repetition Game / Live Contact
- Pinch and Ouch
- Backstory Architecture
- Auditory Point of View

These make the moment feel caused, embodied, lived, and emotionally true.

### D. Coherence Primitives
- Throughline
- Superobjective

These keep the output internally aligned and protect against drift.

### E. Peak / Memory Primitives
- S.T.A.R. Moment

This creates a designed moment of memorability and compression.

### F. Perceptual Guidance Primitives
- Sound as Attention Architecture

This governs what the audience notices first, feels strongest, and remembers most.

## The schema I would propose

I would not use a flat primitive schema.

I would use:

1. a shared **base cell schema**
2. family-specific extensions
3. interaction rules between primitives
4. evaluation objects linked to real outcomes

## 2. Why this schema is closer to the real goal

If the goal is resonance, the schema must answer 5 practical questions:

### What human effect does this primitive produce?
Not theoretically.
Operationally.
Does it increase trust?
Does it increase tension?
Does it create felt recognition?
Does it make the audience lean in?

### When should it fire?
A primitive that works in discovery mode may fail in processing mode.
A primitive that works in a humorous CCF hook may be wrong for a grief-toned Telegram reply.

### What are the risks?
Tribal Reference can create belonging, but also exclusion.
Irony Inversion can produce surprise, but also superiority.
Trojan Horse Narrative can feel elegant, but if overused can feel manipulative.

### What pairs well with it?
Primitives do not work alone.
They create better chemistry in combinations.
For example:

- `Connection Before Content + Tribal Reference`
- `What Is / What Could Be + Throughline`
- `Trojan Horse Narrative + Empathy Inversion`
- `Backstory Architecture + Auditory Point of View`

### How do we evaluate whether it actually worked?
This is the most important part.
If a primitive cannot be scored in outputs and linked to outcomes, it stays as vocabulary rather than infrastructure.

## 4. The master scoring dimensions I would use

You said the rating should stay close to the real goal of content that resonates and engages.
I agree.

So I would not make the master primitive score about elegance or theory.
I would score every primitive across these 6 dimensions:

1. `Connection Lift`
How much it increases felt recognition, safety, or belonging.

2. `Surprise Lift`
How much it breaks prediction and opens attention.

3. `Feeling Lift`
How much it increases emotional vividness, pressure, tenderness, relief, tension, or catharsis.

4. `Memory Lift`
How much it improves recall, residue, or retellability.

5. `Action Lift`
How much it improves readiness to reply, save, share, continue, act, or commit.

6. `Misuse Risk`
How easily it becomes manipulative, forced, alienating, or synthetic.

That gives us a much more honest profile than one flat score.

## 6. Best way to include these in CCP

I would not start by adding all 16 as runtime generation fields.

I would include them in this order:

### First layer: evaluation
Use them to build validators first.
That keeps the ontology honest.

### Second layer: receipts
Label strong and weak outputs with primitive presence and primitive quality.

### Third layer: JIT reasoning
Let the system choose a primitive mix based on mood, archetype, format, and goal.

### Fourth layer: training
Once the receipts are stable, use them for SFT, steering, DPO, or reward shaping.

This order matters because primitives should first become **measurable**, then **routable**, then **trainable**.

---

## Final recommendation

If I had to reduce this into one sentence, it would be:

**the right primitive schema for CCP is not a dictionary of techniques, but a cell-based system that models how each primitive changes human state, under what conditions, on which surface, with which risks, and in which combinations.**

That gets much closer to your standard:

- build connection
- surprise them
- make them feel something

And honestly, I think that is the right standard.
