# Primitive Family Classification for Current CCP and CMF Workflows

## 1. Purpose

This document classifies the primitive library by **actual workflow usefulness** inside the current CCP ecosystem, not by book genre.

It should now be read as a **workflow scan surface**, not as the whole primitive ontology.

That means:

- it helps operators see which primitive families matter at which workflow stages
- it helps identify build order and family relevance quickly
- it does **not** replace the split between `meaning primitives`, `experience primitives`, and `crosswalked territory`

The goal is to answer four practical questions:

1. Which primitive families matter most at each current workflow stage?
2. In what order should those families be used across CCP and CMF?
3. Which families deserve the earliest operational hard-coding based on MCDA?
4. Where are the major overlaps, so we avoid false duplication in the registry?

## 2. Current Workflow Map

The current system naturally breaks into nine meaningful use-case categories.

### Category 1. Research and CRAL Signal Discovery

Use case:
- identify broad but charged primary signals
- detect latent tensions worth bringing to the coach
- avoid generic topical research

### Category 2. Trigger-First Provocation and Coach Inbox Design

Use case:
- bring the right question, contrast, or observation to the coach
- provoke authentic reaction without over-steering

### Category 3. Authenticated Capture and Raw Truth Access

Use case:
- help the coach produce usable, emotionally live source material
- reduce performance and raise signal density

### Category 4. Blueprint Distillation and Coalition Formation

Use case:
- transform authentic material into structured content possibility
- detect primitive candidates and route strong coalitions

### Category 5. CCF Script Architecture and Multi-Format Content

Use case:
- build hooks, bodies, proof sequences, and CTA logic
- keep pieces coherent and anti-centroid across formats

### Category 6. CMF Narrative Extraction and Beat Clustering

Use case:
- turn testimonial or transcript material into cinematic narrative form
- detect movement, quote hierarchy, and emotional turn logic

### Category 7. CMF Visual, Motion, and Sonic Composition

Use case:
- route narrative into storyboard, motion, music, ambient, and visual timing
- control salience, rhythm, reveal, and emotional pacing

### Category 8. CBCS / Telegram / Voice / Speaking Coaching

Use case:
- make one-to-one communication feel human, relieving, and alive
- improve trust, delivery, intimacy, and vocal believability

### Category 9. V2WS, Conversion, and Persuasion Events

Use case:
- move people through long-form guidance, invitation, and buying transitions
- coordinate trust, tension, desire, and action

## 3. The 10 Primitive Families

The audit library supports ten working `meaning-oriented` families for the current workflow architecture.

This is important:

- these families are still useful for research, routing, script building, CMF composition, speaking, and persuasion scanning
- but they should not be confused with the `experience primitive families` used for onboarding, score reveals, sharing loops, comeback logic, replay, and conversion

### Family A. Story Discovery

What it does:
- helps find truthful material before assembly
- reduces overwhelm
- increases signal density
- improves authenticity before formatting

Representative primitives:
- Bird-by-Bird Framing
- Shitty First Drafts
- Polaroid Development Model
- Broccoli vs KFKD
- Moral Point of View
- Unsilenced Voice
- Memory Mining Through Story Seeds
- Stakes as Personal Why

### Family B. Connection and Relational Attunement

What it does:
- creates safety, recognition, belonging, and permission

- Warmth-Before-Competence
- Audience-as-Hero
- Sympathy Engineering
- Matching Principle
- Looping for Understanding
- Trust-Transfer Ladder

### Family C. Contrast and Tension

## 4. Workflow Order by Category

This section ranks the families by actual usefulness inside each current CCP/CMF category.

## 4.1 Category 1 — Research and CRAL Signal Discovery

Order:
1. Psychological Diagnostics and Internal-State Engineering
2. Story Discovery
3. Contrast and Tension
4. Explanation and Translation
5. Connection and Relational Attunement
6. Humor Distortion and Pattern Break

Why:
- diagnostics determine if the material even maps to the client's internal script
- research must then surface truthful material
- then find where the strongest pressure lives
- then compress that into a coach-relevant signal

## 4.2 Category 2 — Trigger-First Provocation and Coach Inbox Design

Order:
1. Psychological Diagnostics and Internal-State Engineering
2. Contrast and Tension
3. Connection and Relational Attunement
4. Story Discovery
5. Humor Distortion and Pattern Break
6. Explanation and Translation

Why:
- inbox design must be informed by which conversation layer the coach is currently on
- the prompt needs charge first
- but not so much charge that it destroys trust
- humor can help, but only after the signal is already valid

## 4.3 Category 3 — Authenticated Capture and Raw Truth Access

Order:
1. Psychological Diagnostics and Internal-State Engineering
2. Story Discovery
3. Performance and Delivery
4. Connection and Relational Attunement
5. Contrast and Tension
6. Explanation and Translation

Why:
- raw truth access depends entirely on the coach's ego state and internal regulation
- clean source material depends on access, not polish
- voice and emotional permission matter before content shaping

## 4.4 Category 4 — Blueprint Distillation and Coalition Formation

Order:
1. Contrast and Tension
2. Narrative Structure and Movement
3. Explanation and Translation
4. Story Discovery
5. Persuasion and Motivation Architecture

Why:
- this is where movement and edge become formal
- primitive candidates need tension and structural routeability

## 4.5 Category 5 — CCF Script Architecture and Multi-Format Content

Order:
1. Narrative Structure and Movement
2. Contrast and Tension
3. Connection and Relational Attunement
4. Explanation and Translation
5. Humor Distortion and Pattern Break
6. Persuasion and Motivation Architecture

Why:
- scripts die when they lack movement
- then they die again when they lack contrast
- then they flatten when they lack human closeness

## 4.6 Category 6 — CMF Narrative Extraction and Beat Clustering

Order:
1. Narrative Structure and Movement
2. Story Discovery
3. Contrast and Tension
4. Performance and Delivery
5. Visual and Sonic Guidance

Why:
- CMF starts from transformation and sequence
- it needs meaning movement before visual style

## 4.7 Category 7 — CMF Visual, Motion, and Sonic Composition

Order:
1. Visual and Sonic Guidance
2. Narrative Structure and Movement
3. Performance and Delivery
4. Contrast and Tension
5. Explanation and Translation

Why:
- salience, rhythm, reveal, and emotional pacing rule this layer
- structure still matters, but it arrives in cinematic form

## 4.8 Category 8 — CBCS / Telegram / Voice / Speaking Coaching

Order:
1. Psychological Diagnostics and Internal-State Engineering
2. Connection and Relational Attunement
3. Performance and Delivery
4. Story Discovery
5. Humor Distortion and Pattern Break
6. Contrast and Tension
7. Persuasion and Motivation Architecture
8. Visual and Sonic Guidance

Why:
- voice coaching requires active stress pot and script monitoring
- safety and felt humanness come next
- humor helps as a tension release bypass
- then delivery quality (using Voice intimacy and ear-writing mechanics)
- then content force

## 4.9 Category 9 — V2WS, Conversion, and Persuasion Events

Order:
1. Persuasion and Motivation Architecture
2. Connection and Relational Attunement
3. Narrative Structure and Movement
4. Humor Distortion and Pattern Break
5. Contrast and Tension
6. Performance and Delivery
7. Explanation and Translation

Why:
- this is where invitation, trust, and action readiness must align
- comedy-as-bypass (Trojan Horse) acts as a powerful conversion tool
- structured trust-transfer and commercial reframing (from Referral Primitives) drive the actual conversion event

## 5. MCDA Framework for Family Prioritization

Each family is scored from `0-200` using four dimensions. Scoring is explicitly differentiated with steeper penalties for narrow cross-system reach or poor measurement readiness to ensure a meaningful spread.

- `Workflow Centrality (50)`:
  How many current CCP/CMF stages depend heavily on this family.

- `Cross-System Reach (50)`:
  How strongly the family transfers across CCF, CMF, CBCS, Voice, Telegram, and V2WS.

- `Differentiation / Anti-Slop Power (50)`:
  How much this family helps CCP avoid centroid output and create premium signal.

- `Measurement Readiness (50)`:
  How realistically this family can be packetized, validated, benchmarked, or routed now.

## 8. Overlap Report

Overlaps are present, and they should be made explicit so the registry does not duplicate functionality under different names.

## 8.1 Contrast and Humor Distortion

This is the strongest overlap zone.

Examples:
- `Irony Inversion`
- `Expectation Reversal`
- `Rule of Three`
- `Misplaced Focus`
- `What-Is / What-Could-Be`

Shared mechanism:
- expectation hardens
- frame is violated
- attention spikes

Difference:
- the `Contrast` family is broader and can be serious, transformational, or persuasive
- the `Humor Distortion` family uses distortion and release more explicitly

Registry rule:
- do not merge them fully
- store shared ancestry notes
- mark many humor primitives as specializations of contrast behavior

## 8.2 Story Discovery and Narrative Structure

Examples:
- `One-Sentence Lens`
- `Throughline`
- `Promise of the Premise`
- `Bird-by-Bird`
- `Memory Mining`
- `Stakes as Personal Why`

Shared mechanism:
- reduce chaos into a coherent center

Difference:
- `Story Discovery` is upstream and helps find the material
- `Narrative Structure` is downstream and helps organize the material

Registry rule:
- keep them separate
- mark cross-links heavily

## 8.3 Connection and Persuasion

Examples:
- `Warmth-Before-Competence`
- `Connection Before Content`
- `Audience-as-Hero`
- `Sympathy Engineering`
- `Teaching as Compassion`
- `Logos-Ethos-Pathos`

Shared mechanism:
- lower resistance and increase permission to receive

Difference:
- `Connection` focuses on trust, safety, belonging
- `Persuasion` focuses on movement, belief, action

Registry rule:
- no merge
- add a note that many persuasion primitives require a connection threshold to work properly

## 8.4 Performance and Visual/Sonic Guidance

Examples:
- `Thought Moment Illusion`
- `Auditory Point of View`
- `Multisensory Delivery`
- `Emotionally Competent Stimulus`

Shared mechanism:
- modulate how the message is felt

Difference:
- `Performance` is embodied human delivery
- `Visual/Sonic` is perceptual orchestration across surfaces

Registry rule:
- keep separate
- allow coalition pairing as a default recommendation in CMF and Voice systems

## 8.5 Explanation and Persuasion

Examples:
- `Analogy Bridge`
- `Explanation Engine`
- `Data Point of View`
- `Recommendation Trees`
- `Big Idea`

Shared mechanism:
- create understandable meaning

Difference:
- `Explanation` is primarily about comprehension
- `Persuasion` is about action and belief movement after comprehension

Registry rule:
- explainers should often feed persuasion structures, not replace them

## 8.6 Story Discovery and Psychological Diagnostics

Examples:
- `Narrative Script Flipping`
- `Memory Mining`
- `Bird-by-Bird Framing`

Shared mechanism:
- uncovering hidden material beneath the surface

Difference:
- `Story Discovery` uncovers external content material for assembly
- `Psychological Diagnostics` uncovers internal operating schemas

Registry rule:
- do not merge, but link heavily when the coach is the subject of discovery

## 8.7 Performance and Psychological Diagnostics

Examples:
- `Ego State Switching`
- `Drama Triangle Rotation`
- `Thought Moment Illusion`

Shared mechanism:
- governing the live interaction mechanics

Difference:
- `Psychological Diagnostics` handles the deep ego/conflict state
- `Performance` handles the outward embodied delivery

Registry rule:
- diagnostic primitives are a prerequisite for performance primitives to land effectively

## 8.8 Connection and Psychological Diagnostics

Examples:
- `Matching Principle`
- `Looping for Understanding`
- `Deep Questions`
- `Connection Before Content`

Shared mechanism:
- operating in the relational/interaction space

Difference:
- `Connection` focuses on building warmth, belonging, and permission
- `Psychological Diagnostics` provides real-time detection of the active conversational layer

Registry rule:
- diagnose the layer before deploying connection tools

## 10. Final Strategic Position

The most important insight from classifying the audits against the current workflows is this:

**not all primitive families belong at the same altitude of the system.**

Some are upstream truth-finders.
Some are midstream force-organizers.
Some are downstream amplifiers.

That means the registry should never become one flat library.

The clean stack is:

- `upstream-zero`: Psychological Diagnostics
- `upstream`: Story Discovery, Contrast, Connection
- `midstream`: Narrative Structure, Explanation, Persuasion
- `downstream`: Performance, Visual/Sonic, Humor Distortion

And the strongest overlaps are not bugs.
They are signals of where coalition logic will become most powerful:

- Contrast × Humor
- Story Discovery × Narrative Structure
- Connection × Persuasion
- Performance × Visual/Sonic
- Story Discovery × Psychological Diagnostics
- Performance × Psychological Diagnostics

The real architectural conclusion is:

**CCP should not only benchmark individual primitives. It should benchmark which family combinations survive best at each workflow stage.**

That is where the moat gets much stronger than simple “best archetype” or “best framework” tracking.
