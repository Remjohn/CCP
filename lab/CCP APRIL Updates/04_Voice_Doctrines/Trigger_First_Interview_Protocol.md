# Trigger-First Interview Protocol
**Date:** 2026-05-01  
**Status:** Source of Truth  
**Context:** partner growth, guest acquisition, CMF capture, benchmark intelligence, challenge conversion

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how interviews should operate inside the CCP ecosystem.

The interview is not a generic podcast tactic.
It is not a disguised services pitch.
It is not a replacement for the Offline Field Acceleration Protocol.

It is a **Trigger-First proof and amplification protocol** that:
- captures high-signal human expression
- creates premium content assets fast
- gives partners visible social proof
- gives guests a live taste of the product
- creates challenge entry points
- feeds the system with richer data

## 2. Critical Distinction

This distinction is non-negotiable:

### OFAP
The Offline Field Acceleration Protocol is an **alternative challenge leads acquisition channel**.

Its purpose is:
- help people enter the ecosystem
- using real-world presence, likability, intrigue, and genuine connection

### The Interview Protocol
The interview protocol is the **social proof, content, benchmark, and conversion extension** that sits on top of that ecosystem.

Its purpose is:
- turn human expression into assets
- make partner authority visible
- let guests feel the product
- generate stronger proof for the internet

Short version:

`OFAP gets them in. Interviews make the value visible and transferable.`

## 3. Core Thesis

The interview should be treated as the **first product experience** for many prospects.

Instead of saying:
- here is our platform
- here is our offer
- here is our service

we let the guest experience:
- a meaningful conversation
- a well-researched Trigger-First question flow
- premium edited outputs from their own words
- communication insights or benchmarks
- a natural next step into the free challenge

This is stronger than selling because:
- the guest feels seen
- the guest receives immediate value
- the partner looks strong
- the content proves the engine works

## 4. TRI-Relevance

Every interview must be built around **TRI-Relevance**.

The topic and question architecture must be:

### 4.1 Relevant to the coach
The subject should connect to the partner or host's:
- current positioning
- growth edge
- audience concerns
- communication strengths
- live business direction

### 4.2 Relevant to the audience
The subject should touch:
- real desire
- real tension
- real confusion
- real aspiration
- real emotional charge

It must feel like something the audience already cares about, not a vanity conversation for insiders.

### 4.3 Timely
The subject must matter now.

This timeliness can come from:
- a cultural shift
- a market transition
- a fear the audience is currently living through
- a public conversation
- a seasonal moment
- a topic CRAL identifies as active and resonant

If the interview is not timely, it may still be interesting, but it will not be Trigger-First enough.

## 5. CRAL Pre-Interview Intelligence

Every high-value interview should have a light CRAL pre-pass.

The goal is not to over-academize the interview.
The goal is to identify:
- the live tension worth activating
- the emotional premise worth surfacing
- the contradiction worth exploring
- the language the audience already uses around the topic

Minimum pre-interview CRAL outputs:
- `timely angle`
- `audience tension`
- `coach/guest overlap point`
- `one surprising or contrarian premise`
- `one emotionally loaded question`
- `one question likely to create a memorable reaction`

This is what separates a generic podcast from a CCP interview.

## 6. Interview Roles

### 6.1 Partner or coach host
The host's job is to:
- create safety
- create intrigue
- create momentum
- draw out real speech, not canned monologues

### 6.2 Guest
The guest is not merely an interviewee.
They are:
- a featured voice
- a potential challenge entrant
- a potential referral node
- a possible future client or partner
- a new source of contextual proof

### 6.3 CCP system
The system's job is to:
- capture the long-form expression
- run it through CMF / content generation pathways
- optionally apply voice or speaking benchmarks
- package the outputs fast
- route the guest toward the right next step

## 7. Interview Types

The protocol should support at least 3 interview types.

### Type 1. Authority Collab Interview
Purpose:
- co-create long-form content
- build the partner's status
- generate multiple downstream assets

Best for:
- established coaches
- YouTube collabs
- audience expansion

### Type 2. Triggered Guest Interview
Purpose:
- surface a timely problem or shift
- give the guest visible output and proof
- invite them into the challenge

Best for:
- warm prospects
- coach friends
- founder-led guest acquisition

### Type 3. Audit-Embedded Interview
Purpose:
- create a meaningful conversation
- then use the recording as benchmark or speaking analysis material
- use the results to invite improvement through the challenge

Best for:
- communication-focused offers
- voice coaching
- public speaking growth

## 8. Interview Structure

Recommended structural flow:

### Phase A. Emotional entry
Open with a question that creates relevance quickly.
The guest should feel:
- this is about something real
- this is not a generic show
- I am allowed to say something meaningful here

### Phase B. Tension activation
Use questions that create:
- contrast
- dilemma
- uncertainty
- conviction
- confession

The point is not drama for its own sake.
The point is to pull out living thought rather than polished opinion.

### Phase C. Breakthrough or insight zone
The guest reaches:
- a sharp opinion
- a self-revealing answer
- a memorable story
- a decisive reframing

This is usually where the strongest clips and carousels come from.

### Phase D. Reflection and bridge
Close by making the conversation feel integrated, not abrupt.
Then route naturally into:
- content delivery
- challenge invitation
- benchmark feedback
- follow-up collaboration

## 9. The Content Sequence After The Interview

The post-interview sequence is where the protocol becomes commercially powerful.

### Minimum deliverables
From one strong interview, the guest or partner should quickly receive some combination of:
- reels / shorts
- branded carousel
- quote card or hot take
- humor / meme if appropriate
- longer YouTube or podcast asset

Because CMF already supports long-form transformation, the goal is speed and perceived quality.

The strongest psychological effect is:

`before they even start wondering when the content will be ready, it is already in front of them`

That creates disproportionate wow.

## 10. The Challenge Handoff

The guest should not feel sold.
They should feel upgraded.

The handoff logic is:

1. You were interviewed.
2. You received strong outputs.
3. You saw what the system can do with your real voice.
4. If you want, you can now experience the speaking-growth side of the engine through the free challenge.

This means the invitation should sound like:
- continue the momentum
- experience the full system
- improve your own communication
- see what changes in 7 days

Not:
- buy our service
- book a demo
- let us sell you content

## 11. Why IRL Interviews Matter

IRL interviews should be strongly encouraged and eventually trained for partners.

Why:
- better body language data
- better atmosphere
- stronger status transfer
- more natural chemistry
- richer B-roll and visual proof
- better raw material for social proof

When affordable and practical, IRL is superior because it produces more of the exact human-first evidence the brand needs.

But this must remain clear:

IRL interviews are not required for the whole business model to work.
They are a premium extension and force multiplier.

## 12. Data Value

An interview is more than content.
It is high-quality first-party signal.

It gives the system:
- voice
- cadence
- timing
- worldview
- emotional spikes
- objections
- narrative habits
- authority markers

That data is structurally superior to:
- a cold signup
- a bio form
- a random lead magnet opt-in

This is why interview-led growth fits CCP so well.

## 13. Partner Responsibilities

If a partner uses this protocol, they should be accountable for:
- hosting interviews consistently
- using the system themselves
- sharing outputs publicly
- inviting aligned guests, not random vanity guests
- routing guests to the challenge where appropriate

The protocol is strongest when partners are not passive recommenders.
They should be active media operators inside the ecosystem.

## 14. KPI Layer

Recommended metrics:
- interviews hosted per month
- asset package delivery speed
- number of guest outputs posted
- guest challenge opt-in rate
- guest continuation rate after challenge
- partner audience growth from collabs
- speaking benchmark completion rate
- referrals generated from past guests

These metrics matter because they convert the partner system from vague goodwill into measurable operating behavior.

## 15. Final Rule

The Trigger-First Interview Protocol exists to make sure the long-form conversation is never empty.

It must always do multiple jobs at once:
- create relationship
- create content
- create proof
- create insight
- create a challenge entry path
- create data

If an interview only produces "awareness," it is underperforming.

The CCP standard is higher:

**every interview should be a proof-producing human event that deepens trust, upgrades perception, and creates the next meaningful step in the ecosystem.**
