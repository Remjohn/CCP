## II. Introduction: Methodology & Goal

### The Goal

We have spent 11 deep-research sessions extracting hyper-growth strategies from the world's most effective founders, investors, and operators. The raw output is 17 documents containing over 200 discrete strategic insights. This abundance is a liability if not disciplined. The purpose of this MCDA is to mathematically compress 200+ insights into exactly **23 candidate features**, score them against **8 non-negotiable Sovereign Growth Criteria** using **CBAR and TRIZ reasoning** over each criterion, and surface the **Top 16 features** that will be operationalized into the CCP PRD.

Features that do not make the Top 16 are not "killed" — they are archived in a backlog. But the CCP's engineering bandwidth is finite. We must ruthlessly prioritize the features that maximize our ability to grow fast, become impossible to leave, deliver AI-assisted human transformation, create addictive user experiences, and generate zero-CAC viral loops.

### The Methodology

1. **Feature Extraction:** 23 discrete, actionable platform features were extracted from the 17-document corpus. Each feature is traceable to its source document(s).
2. **Criterion Definition:** 8 Sovereign Growth Criteria were established, each with a maximum point value, totaling 200. Criteria are weighted by strategic priority — Sunk-Cost Defensibility is worth more than Asymmetric Time Leverage because retention compounds faster than efficiency.
3. **CBAR Stress Test (Per Criterion):** For each criterion, we surface the core adversarial tension — the scenario in which a feature *appears* to score high but fails under real-world constraint pressure. This prevents score inflation.
4. **TRIZ Resolution (Per Criterion):** For each criterion, we apply TRIZ inventive principles to resolve the contradiction identified by CBAR, ensuring the scoring system captures genuine value, not theoretical promise.
5. **Scoring:** Each of the 23 features is scored 0 to MAX on each criterion. Scores are summed. The Top 16 features are selected.
6. **Validation:** The Top 16 are cross-checked against the PRD to ensure architectural feasibility.

## III. The 8 Sovereign Growth Criteria: Definitions, CBAR, and TRIZ

### Criterion 1: Sunk-Cost Defensibility (SCD) — Max 35 Points

**Definition:** Does using this feature create irreversible switching costs? Once the coach has invested time, data, and identity into this feature, does leaving CCP mean losing something they cannot reconstruct elsewhere?

**Why 35 Points:** Retention is the single highest-leverage variable in a subscription business. A coach who churns at Month 3 costs more in acquisition than they ever paid. A coach who *cannot* churn because their entire business infrastructure lives inside CCP is worth $2,400/year in perpetuity. Every other criterion is subordinate to defensibility.

**CBAR Tension:** A feature can *feel* sticky without actually being sticky. If CCP stores a coach's data but the data is easily exportable (CSV download of client names), the perceived lock-in is high but the actual switching cost is near-zero. The adversarial challenge: *Can a competitor replicate, import, or bypass the sunk cost in under 48 hours?*

**TRIZ Resolution (Principle 10 — Prior Action):** True sunk-cost defensibility must be *compounding* — the value of the feature must increase with every week of use. A static database is exportable. A dynamic intelligence graph that has learned the coach's voice patterns, mapped 5,000 client interactions, and built a behavioral model that predicts churn before it happens — that cannot be exported because the *intelligence itself* is the product of accumulated time. We score highest on SCD when the feature generates **irreproducible compounding intelligence**, not merely stored data.

**Scoring Rule:** 35 = Coach's entire business would collapse if feature were removed. 25 = Significant disruption, 6+ months to rebuild. 15 = Moderate inconvenience. 5 = Easily replaced by competitor.

---

### Criterion 2: Viral Coefficient (VC) — Max 30 Points

**Definition:** Does using this feature naturally and involuntarily create exposure to new potential customers? Not "Can the coach share it?" but "Does the feature's *normal operation* force organic referrals, social proof, or brand visibility?"

**Why 30 Points:** Paid acquisition is a tax. Viral acquisition is compound interest. If the viral coefficient k > 1.0, every user acquired generates >1 additional user. Our target market (coaches) are inherently social creatures — they live in communities, they cross-promote, they attend events. The platform must weaponize this.

**CBAR Tension:** "Shareable" and "viral" are different. A coach can *share* a beautifully branded carousel, but if nobody who sees it understands what produced it or how to get their own, the sharing is dead-end virality — it promotes the coach, not CCP. The adversarial challenge: *Does the feature create a visible, traceable trail back to the platform?*

**TRIZ Resolution (Principle 24 — Intermediary):** The feature must make the *platform* visible through the coach's use without requiring the coach to actively promote CCP. The intermediary is the artifact — a branded visual with a subtle "Produced by CCP" watermark, a Trivianar event that structurally requires a Telegram bot link, a biometric scorecard that only exists inside the CCP ecosystem. The trail is architectural, not promotional.

**Scoring Rule:** 30 = Every use inherently exposes CCP to new users (k > 1). 20 = Sharing is natural but requires coach action. 10 = Shareable but no platform trail. 0 = Purely internal utility.

---

### Criterion 3: Denominator Suppression (DS) — Max 30 Points

**Definition:** Does this feature eliminate an existing cost, hire, or overhead line item from the coach's P&L? Not "Does it add value?" but "Does it *kill an expense*?"

**Why 30 Points:** Graham Weaver at Alpine Investors proved: wealth = numerator minus denominator. Most coaches operate at 30–40% margins because they pay for video editors ($1,500/mo), VAs ($800/mo), scheduling tools ($200/mo), and course platforms ($300/mo). If CCP can replace multiple line items, the coach's effective ROI on our $200/mo subscription is 10:1 before we even discuss revenue generation.

**CBAR Tension:** Replacement is only valuable if the quality matches or exceeds the incumbent. If CCP's automated video editing is 70% as good as a human editor, the coach will keep paying the human editor *and* paying for CCP — increasing their denominator. The adversarial challenge: *Would the coach confidently fire their current provider the week they onboard CCP?*

**TRIZ Resolution (Principle 2 — Extraction):** The feature must not try to replicate the *entire* service a human provides. It must extract the specific high-value output (the finished video, the branded carousel, the scheduled post) while eliminating the human overhead (the briefing calls, the revision cycles, the quality inconsistency). We score highest when the feature provides *better* output than the incumbent at *zero* marginal human cost.

**Scoring Rule:** 30 = Eliminates $1,000+/mo in existing expenses. 20 = Eliminates $500–$1,000/mo. 10 = Reduces friction but doesn't eliminate a hire. 0 = Adds capability without replacing cost.

---

### Criterion 4: Algorithmic Intimacy (AI) — Max 25 Points

**Definition:** Does this feature simulate, enhance, or deliver the feeling of deep human connection at scale — making the client feel individually seen, heard, and understood even when the coach is not personally present?

**Why 25 Points:** The coaching industry's entire value proposition is intimacy. A PDF does not change lives. A voice note from someone who knows your name, remembers your struggle, and speaks to your specific obstacle *does*. If CCP can deliver this feeling 24/7 while the coach sleeps, we have built the ultimate "Digital Stunt Double" (Lemkin) — and the coach becomes dependent on CCP for the most valuable thing they sell.

**CBAR Tension:** "Personalized" AI can feel uncanny. If the AI coach sends a message clearly referencing your data but with a robotic, templated structure, the client feels surveilled, not seen. The adversarial challenge: *Would the client believe this came from the actual coach if there were no "AI" disclosure?*

**TRIZ Resolution (Principle 13 — The Other Way Round):** Instead of making AI *pretend* to be the coach (which fails the uncanny valley), design the feature so the AI is explicitly an extension of the coach — "Coach X's Intelligence System" — and make the *quality of insight* so high that the client doesn't care about the medium. The client values accuracy over authenticity of channel. A RAG-trained agent that delivers a perfect psychological insight beats a hurried, distracted human response.

**Scoring Rule:** 25 = Client cannot distinguish from human coach attention. 18 = High personalization with visible AI scaffolding. 10 = Generic personalization (name insertion). 0 = No intimacy component.

---

### Criterion 5: Gamified Novelty (GN) — Max 25 Points

**Definition:** Does this feature create dopamine loops, progress tracking, competitive mechanics, or variable rewards that make the user want to return daily — not because they "should" but because they *can't help it*?

**Why 25 Points:** SaaS tools die from boredom. A coach logs into Kajabi once a week, stares at a flat dashboard, and feels nothing. The coaching industry has zero gamification precedent. If CCP can make the coach's daily workflow feel like a game — with scores improving, badges unlocking, leaderboards shifting — we become the first platform to engineer *addiction* in the B2B coaching space.

**CBAR Tension:** Gamification can feel patronizing to high-ticket professionals. A $10,000/month executive coach being told they "earned a gold star" will churn instantly. The adversarial challenge: *Does the gamification feel like a video game for children, or like a status economy for apex predators?*

**TRIZ Resolution (Principle 17 — Another Dimension):** Shift the gamification dimension from "fun badges" to "biometric precision." Instead of "You earned a streak badge!" the system says: "Your prosody variance dropped 23% this week. You are now in the top 8% of coaches on the platform." The game is not about collecting trinkets — it is about watching your own measurable performance increase against a population benchmark. This is gamification for Type-A achievers.

**Scoring Rule:** 25 = Daily return is psychologically compulsive. 18 = Weekly return driven by progress curiosity. 10 = Occasional novelty. 0 = Static tool with no engagement loop.

---

### Criterion 6: Deployment Velocity (DV) — Max 20 Points

**Definition:** Can the coach activate, launch, or benefit from this feature within seconds to minutes — not hours, days, or weeks?

**Why 20 Points:** Pieter Levels ships features in 20 seconds. Our coaches are not developers. If a feature requires a 45-minute setup wizard, a Zoom onboarding call, or a 3-week content audit before the coach sees value, we have already lost them. The "12 Minutes a Day" promise means every feature must deliver within a micro-session window.

**CBAR Tension:** Speed can sacrifice quality. If the Skia renderer produces a carousel in 4 seconds but the typography is misaligned, the coach loses trust faster than they gained convenience. The adversarial challenge: *Is the first output production-ready, or does it require human review and correction?*

**TRIZ Resolution (Principle 35 — Parameter Change):** The feature must be designed so that the "default" output is already excellent. Instead of offering 50 customization options that slow deployment, the system makes intelligent defaults based on the coach's Voice DNA, brand palette, and content history. The coach clicks once. The output is 95% perfect. If they want to adjust the remaining 5%, they can — but they never have to.

**Scoring Rule:** 20 = Value delivered in under 60 seconds. 15 = Value delivered in under 5 minutes. 10 = Value delivered in under 1 hour. 0 = Requires multi-day setup.

---

### Criterion 7: Authority Engineering (AE) — Max 20 Points

**Definition:** Does this feature position the coach as an undeniable expert, thought leader, or status figure in their niche — making their audience perceive them as categorically above competitors?

**Why 20 Points:** Coaches compete on perceived authority. The one who *looks* like the authority gets the clients, regardless of actual competence. If CCP can engineer the perception of authority through cinematic content, biometric transparency, verified dashboards, and Hollywood-level storytelling — we become the factory that produces industry leaders.

**CBAR Tension:** Authority engineering can backfire if the coach's actual competence doesn't match the produced perception. If the content makes a mediocre coach look like Tony Robbins, early clients will churn when they experience the reality gap. The adversarial challenge: *Does the feature inflate perception or genuinely develop the coach's actual capability?*

**TRIZ Resolution (Principle 22 — Blessing in Disguise):** The highest-authority features are the ones that *simultaneously* improve the coach's real skill while broadcasting that improvement. The Voice Coach doesn't just make them *look* better — it makes them *speak* better. The biometric scorecard doesn't just create perception of expertise — it creates actual expertise. Authority engineering that compounds real skill is immune to the reality-gap problem.

**Scoring Rule:** 20 = Simultaneously builds real skill and broadcasts authority. 15 = Strong authority signal, moderate skill development. 10 = Authority signal only. 0 = Purely internal utility.

---

### Criterion 8: Asymmetric Time Leverage (ATL) — Max 15 Points

**Definition:** Does this feature deliver disproportionate output relative to the time invested? Specifically, does it convert 12 minutes of coach input into 12+ hours of platform-generated value?

**Why 15 Points (Lowest Weight):** Time leverage is important but subordinate to the other criteria because it is a *prerequisite*, not a *differentiator*. Every AI tool promises time savings. Time leverage alone does not create defensibility, virality, or intimacy. It is the necessary condition for a feature to function within the "12 Minutes a Day" mandate, but insufficient by itself.

**CBAR Tension:** A feature that saves time but produces low-quality output is worse than doing nothing — it creates technical debt (bad content published, reputation damaged). The adversarial challenge: *Is the time savings genuine, or does the coach spend the "saved" time fixing the output?*

**TRIZ Resolution (Principle 25 — Self-Service):** The feature must be self-correcting. If the AI produces a script, it must also validate the script against the coach's Voice DNA before surfacing it. The coach should never receive output that requires fixing. Time leverage must be measured as *net* time saved — total time previously required minus total time currently required (including review and correction).

**Scoring Rule:** 15 = 12 minutes input → 12+ hours autonomous value. 10 = Significant time savings (5:1 ratio). 5 = Moderate time savings (2:1 ratio). 0 = No meaningful time leverage.
