# **The Deep Architecture of Normative Conflict: Moral Foundations Theory and the Values Trigger Architecture**

The fundamental challenge in modern behavioral analysis and professional coaching is the "observational trap," a systemic limitation where triggers are identified by their surface-level manifestations—what a subject responds to—rather than the structural psychological causes—why they respond to it. The emergence of the Values Trigger Architecture, underpinned by Jonathan Haidt’s Moral Foundations Theory (MFT), addresses this gap by mapping the deep architecture of the human moral mind. This architecture provides the scientific framework for the Norm Compatibility Threshold, the specific point at which external stimuli interact with internal "moral taste receptors" to produce visceral emotional responses. By moving from a descriptive model of behavior to a predictive model of normative activation, this architecture allows for the identification of authentic material before it is even articulated by the subject.

## **The Epistemological Shift from Observation to Prediction**

In traditional behavioral monitoring, a coach or mentor observes a client’s reaction to a specific event—such as a policy change or an institutional constraint—and tags it as a trigger. This methodology is inherently reactive; it requires the event to occur and the reaction to be recorded before any insight is gained. However, the integration of Moral Foundations Theory into a digital engine allows for a transition toward structural prediction. This shift is predicated on the understanding that human morality is not a monolithic rational construct but a set of modular, innate systems shaped by evolutionary pressures.1

These systems, which Haidt identifies as six universal moral foundations, function as an "audio equalizer," where each individual has adjusted the sliders to different levels.3 A coach with a high weighting on the Liberty/Oppression foundation will respond to an institutional mandate with immediate reactance and outrage, whereas a coach weighted toward Care/Harm might view the same mandate as a necessary protection for the vulnerable.4 The fact of the mandate remains constant; the emotional response varies because different moral taste receptors are firing. Understanding these settings allows a behavioral engine to stop waiting for reactions and start forecasting which current events will ignite the most authentic and high-energy material from a specific subject.

## **The Evolutionary Scaffolding of the Moral Mind**

Moral Foundations Theory posits that the human mind is born with a "first draft" of morality, a collection of intuitive systems that provided evolutionary advantages to our ancestors as they navigated social dilemmas.6 This draft is subsequently revised by culture and individual experience, but the underlying modularity remains. These foundations are categorized into "individualizing" clusters, which focus on the rights and welfare of individuals, and "binding" clusters, which focus on group cohesion and social order.1

### **The Care/Harm Foundation**

The Care/Harm foundation is rooted in the long evolution of mammals with attachment systems. Specifically, it developed from the necessity of mothers to detect and respond to the suffering or distress of their offspring.3 Over time, this sensitivity has expanded from kin to broader social groups, manifesting as virtues of kindness, gentleness, and nurturance.5 When this foundation is triggered, it produces an immediate affective response of compassion for the victim and outrage toward the perpetrator of harm. In a professional context, a coach high in this foundation will be predictably activated by any topic involving perceived cruelty or the neglect of vulnerable populations, regardless of the institutional justification.4

### **The Fairness/Cheating Foundation**

The Fairness/Cheating foundation evolved in response to the adaptive challenge of reciprocal altruism. For groups to function without being exploited by "free riders," individuals needed to develop a sensitivity to who was contributing and who was cheating.9 This foundation underlies the values of justice, rights, and autonomy. Critically, research has demonstrated that "fairness" is not a singular concept. While some view it through the lens of Equality (everyone receiving the same), others view it through Proportionality (rewards matching contributions).1 This distinction is vital for a predictive engine; a coach who prioritizes proportionality will be triggered by "fairness-based" welfare systems that they perceive as disincentivizing work, while an equality-focused coach will be triggered by the systemic wealth gaps those same systems fail to bridge.9

### **The Loyalty/Betrayal Foundation**

The Loyalty/Betrayal foundation is a product of our history as tribal creatures who lived in competing coalitions. To survive, individuals had to be able to form and maintain strong group bonds, which required a mechanism for identifying and punishing betrayal.5 This foundation underlies patriotism, self-sacrifice for the group, and a visceral hatred for "traitors" who abandon the team. In the Values Trigger Architecture, a coach whose corpus shows repeated mentions of institutional betrayal—such as industry bodies that claim to represent practitioners but instead extract value for themselves—is flagged as having a high Loyalty weighting.11 Any new event that fits this "betrayal template" will predictably produce authentic, high-valence material from that coach.

### **The Authority/Subversion Foundation**

Authority/Subversion was shaped by the hierarchical nature of primate social interactions. It is not merely about power, but about the mutual obligations inherent in leadership and followership.5 This foundation underlies the respect for legitimate tradition, the rule of law, and the maintenance of social order.4 Those with high weighting in this foundation view subversion of authority as a moral failure that risks descent into chaos. Conversely, those with low weighting often view hierarchy as inherently oppressive, a perspective common among political liberals who define themselves in opposition to power structures.9

### **The Sanctity/Degradation Foundation**

The Sanctity/Degradation foundation emerged from the "omnivore’s dilemma" and the need to avoid pathogens and parasites. It is driven by the psychology of disgust and the desire to remain "pure".5 In the moral realm, this has transformed into the belief that the body is a temple that can be desecrated by immoral activities or contaminants.5 While it often manifests in religious contexts, it is also visible in secular movements focused on environmental purity or "toxic-free" living.12 A coach triggered by "degradation" will respond with visceral disgust to topics they perceive as "sleazy," "polluted," or "carnal," even if those topics do not cause direct physical harm.13

### **The Liberty/Oppression Foundation**

Added as a sixth foundation to account for the unique moral profile of libertarians, Liberty/Oppression is rooted in the human tendency to form egalitarian "reverse hierarchies" to suppress alpha-male bullies.1 This foundation triggers a state of psychological "reactance" when an individual perceives their autonomy is being threatened.14 It is the primary driver for those who respond to "institutional constraint" with immediate outrage. While both the left and right utilize this foundation, they do so differently: the left focuses on the liberty of oppressed groups, while the right focuses on liberty from government intrusion.4

## **The Psychometric Instrumentation of Moral Values**

To operationalize these foundations within a technical engine, the Values Trigger Architecture relies on validated psychometric tools that measure an individual's "moral settings." The evolution of these tools reflects a growing sophistication in our understanding of how moral modules interact.

### **The Transition from MFQ to MFQ-2**

The original Moral Foundations Questionnaire (MFQ) was designed to measure endorsement of the first five foundations.16 It utilized two primary metrics: relevance (how important a factor is to your moral judgment) and judgment (agreement with specific moral statements).8 However, as the theory matured, researchers recognized the need for a more granular approach, leading to the development of the MFQ-2.

The MFQ-2, published in 2023, represents a significant refinement in both theory and methodology. It empirically demonstrates that Equality and Proportionality are distinct moral foundations, rather than subsets of a single fairness category.1 This distinction is a major breakthrough for the predictive engine; it allows for the differentiation between a coach who will be triggered by "unfairness" in the form of unequal outcomes and one who will be triggered by "unfairness" in the form of unearned rewards.1 Furthermore, the MFQ-2 has been validated across 25 different populations, ensuring that its predictive power is not limited to Western contexts.11

### **The Higher-Order Structure: Individualizing and Binding Clusters**

Data from the MFQ and MFQ-2 consistently reveal a higher-order structure that organizes these foundations into two clusters. The "individualizing" foundations (Care, Fairness, Liberty) emphasize the protection of the individual, while the "binding" foundations (Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity) emphasize the cohesion of the group.1

This clustering provides a powerful predictive heuristic. If a coach's corpus demonstrates a high sensitivity to one foundation in a cluster, they are statistically more likely to be sensitive to the others in that cluster. For example, a coach who values Authority and tradition is significantly more likely to also value group Loyalty and the Sanctity of shared symbols, such as the national flag or professional credentials.3 This allow the engine to map the "blind spots" of a coach—topics they are likely to ignore because they do not resonate with their primary moral clusters.3

| Cluster | Foundations | Focus | Key Predictor (Ideology) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Individualizing** | Care, Fairness (Equality), Liberty | Protecting/empowering individuals | Highly dominant in Liberals |
| **Binding** | Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity | Cohering/protecting the group | Highly valued by Conservatives |

## **Computational Stylometry: Extracting Values from the Corpus**

The core of the Values Trigger Architecture is the ability to extract these moral foundations from unstructured natural language, such as voice notes, social media posts, or newsletter drafts. This is achieved through a combination of lexicon-based methods and advanced neural network models.

### **The Evolution of Moral Foundations Dictionaries**

The earliest attempts to detect moral sentiment used the Moral Foundations Dictionary (MFD), a collection of word stems associated with the five primary foundations.20 For instance, words like "protect" or "kind" were mapped to Care, while "betray" or "traitor" were mapped to Loyalty.21 However, these dictionaries were static and lacked the ability to handle contextual nuance, such as sarcasm or negation (e.g., "not loyal").21

The development of the MFD 2.0 and the extended MFD (eMFD) addressed these limitations by expanding the word lists and using word-embedding similarity scores to refine the categories.22 By calculating the cosine similarity between a text's vector representation and the "prototypical" vectors of each moral foundation, the engine can bypass the need for explicit word matches and instead capture the "moral flavor" of the discourse.23

### **Neural Representations and ME2-BERT**

The current state-of-the-art in moral foundation extraction is ME2-BERT, a holistic framework that integrates "events" and "emotions" into the prediction process.11 Unlike simple lexicon-based tools, ME2-BERT is fine-tuned to understand the interrelations between what is happening (the event) and how it is being described (the emotion). This model has demonstrated a 35% increase in accuracy for out-of-domain scenarios, making it highly effective at identifying moral foundations in a coach's unique, idiosyncratic voice.11

Furthermore, neuroimaging studies have confirmed that different moral foundations elicit "dissociable neural representations" distributed throughout the cortex.11 When a subject makes a judgment based on Care, different regions of the brain are activated than when they make a judgment based on Authority. This biological evidence supports the claim that the engine is not merely detecting words, but is accessing the "dissociable neural signatures" of the subject's moral modules.11

## **The Values Trigger Map: From Metadata to Instrumentation**

The "Trigger Map" is the operational tool that converts these psychological insights into content-generation strategies. It functions as a "forensic scan" of a subject's moral landscape, mapping their specific self-sabotage signatures and emotional pressure points.24

### **The Mechanism of Normative Activation**

The Values Trigger Map identifies the exact "norm compatibility" of a topic for a specific coach. This is achieved by analyzing the subject’s "Linguistic Fingerprint"—their recurring syntax templates, preferred transition patterns, and idiosyncratic micro-habits.25 When a subject is activated by a moral trigger, their language changes; the register becomes more intense, the vocabulary shifts toward "virtue and vice" terms, and the sentence structure often becomes more rhythmic or "musical".21

The engine uses this information to build a "Replication Blueprint" for the coach. It identifies the "red lines" that the coach will never cross and the "degrees of freedom" where they are most expressive.25 For a coach high in Liberty/Oppression, a "red line" might be any content that implies a need for government or institutional oversight. By avoiding these dead zones and targeting the active foundations, the engine ensures the material produced is consistently high-energy and authentic.

### **Predicting the "Strongest Authentic Material"**

The transformative power of this architecture is its ability to forecast the "authentic response" to current events. The engine defines trigger events in behavioral terms and tags them within its strategy builder.26

1. **Event Analysis:** A new industry regulation is announced that mandates specific disclosure requirements for coaches.  
2. **Structural Mapping:** The engine identifies this as an "Institutional Constraint" event. It checks the subject's moral profile.  
3. **Conflict Identification:** Subject A has a high Liberty weighting. Subject B has a high Care weighting.  
4. **Forecasting:** The engine predicts that Subject A will experience "immediate visceral outrage" and will likely produce a rant on "the death of professional freedom." It predicts that Subject B will be indifferent or mildly supportive, seeing it as a way to "protect clients from bad actors".14

By feeding Subject A prompts that lean into their Liberty outrage, the engine elicits material that feels deeply authentic to their brand and values, rather than generic industry commentary.

## **Ideological Profiling and the Logic of Reactance**

The Values Trigger Architecture is particularly effective at distinguishing between the different psychological "logics" of major ideological groups. Research led by Ravi Iyer has provided the most detailed datasets in the world on these variations, particularly concerning the unique profile of libertarians.15

### **The Libertarian Profile: Moralized Rationality**

Libertarians exhibit a psychological profile that is distinct from both the traditional left and right. They match liberals in their low valuation of the "binding" foundations (Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity) but join conservatives in their lower valuation of Care and Fairness (Equality).15 Their moral center of gravity is almost entirely focused on individual Liberty.

Furthermore, libertarians show a "systemizing" rather than "empathizing" cognitive style. They are more likely to analyze the underlying rules of a system than to respond to the emotional distress of individuals within that system.15 This makes them highly sensitive to "systemic contradictions" in institutional policies. If a coach is flagged as having this "Moralized Rationality," the engine knows to trigger them with examples of institutional hypocrisy or logical fallacies in government mandates, which will produce a highly intellectualized, yet visceral, defense of autonomy.27

### **The Liberal Profile: Care-Based Universalism**

In contrast, political liberals prioritize Care and Fairness (Equality) above all else. Their morality is "individualizing," but in a universalist sense—they tend toward a concern for all people, rather than just their own in-group.12 This often leads to a "blindness" toward the binding foundations. When a conservative speaks of Loyalty or Authority, a liberal often hears only "theta-waves"—it sounds like nonsense to them because they lack the moral taste receptors for those specific frequencies.3

### **The Conservative Profile: The Social Matrix**

Conservatives utilize a broader "moral spectrum," weighting all six foundations relatively equally.3 This provides them with a "social matrix" that prioritizes the stability of institutions and the preservation of traditions. They are uniquely triggered by "Sanctity" violations—such as the desecration of flags or religious symbols—that a liberal or libertarian would likely view as protected speech or harmless behavior.5

| **Liberal** | Care, Equality | Suffering, systemic bias | Empathetic, activist |
| **Conservative** | All 6 (Binding focus) | Subversion, betrayal, filth | Protective, traditionalist |

## **Institutional Constraint and the Norm Compatibility Threshold**

A central variable in the Proposal 3 framework is the "Norm Compatibility Threshold"—the specific point at which a coach’s internal values make a topic or constraint either "compatible" or "incompatible" with their worldview. This threshold is most clearly visible in how coaches respond to institutional pressure.

### **The Liberty vs. Care Experiment**

Consider an experiment where a professional body imposes a new "code of ethics" that limits the types of claims a coach can make.

* **The Liberty-Weighted Coach:** Their Norm Compatibility Threshold is low for any form of external constraint. They perceive the code as a "massive curtailment of liberty".28 Their emotional response is outrage, and their content will focus on the "tyranny of the board" and the "right to speak one's truth."  
* **The Care-Weighted Coach:** Their threshold is high; they are willing to accept the constraint if it prevents harm. They only respond with outrage if they see the code *causing* harm (e.g., preventing a helpful but non-traditional technique from being used).16

The engine recognizes that "the same fact produces completely different emotional responses" in these two individuals \[User Query\]. By understanding the "why"—the moral module firing—the engine can predict exactly where a coach will "draw the line" in the sand.29

### **The "Taboo Tradeoff" Metric**

The engine can further quantify this threshold using the "Taboo Tradeoff" questionnaire. This metric asks participants how much money they would require to violate a specific moral foundation (e.g., "betray a friend" or "kick a dog").16 Some individuals refuse to perform the action for any amount of money, indicating that the foundation is "sacred" and non-negotiable. If a coach has a "sacred" value around Loyalty, the engine knows that any event suggesting a breach of loyalty will produce an explosive, uncompromised reaction.10

## **Global and Cross-Cultural Implementation**

The Values Trigger Architecture must account for the fact that moral "taste buds" are not distributed equally across the globe. The "audio equalizer" is set differently in different cultures, which affects how coaches in different regions will respond to the same global trends.3

### **Cultural Variants: The Concept of Qeirat**

In certain Middle Eastern cultures, the concept of "Qeirat" encompasses honor, the defense of family, and national loyalty.1 Research has shown that Qeirat is strongly correlated with the "binding" foundations—Loyalty, Authority, and Purity—and acts as a powerful moral driver that is often absent in Western-centric models.1 An engine analyzing a coach from this region would need to adjust its Trigger Map to account for this unique "honor-based" module.

### **Regional "Audio Equalizer" Settings**

Cross-cultural validation studies have shown that world region is a significant predictor of foundation scores, even when controlling for age and gender.16

* **Japan:** Communication is often mediated by a trade-off between Fairness and Authority foundations.30  
* **Sweden/Western Europe:** Shows a high degree of scalar invariance, but with a pronounced "lifestyle liberty" factor that is distinct from "government liberty".31  
* **Iran:** Demonstrates that the newly developed MFQ-2 is highly effective at capturing the interconnectivity of moral foundations in non-Western, developing contexts.32

| Region | Primary Foundations | Cultural Nuance |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Western (WEIRD)** | Care, Fairness (Equality) | High universalism, low binding |
| **Japan** | Fairness, Authority | Trade-off between order and equity |
| **Middle East** | Loyalty, Authority, Purity | "Qeirat" (Honor/Defense) |
| **Latin America** | Loyalty, Care | Strong group bonds and nurturance |

This instrumentation ensures that the pipeline never stalls because the triggers are no longer "trivia"—they are measurable events with expected downstream behaviors.26

## **Conclusion: The Future of Morally-Aware Coaching Systems**

The Values Trigger Architecture represents the next frontier in professional development and communication. By filling the gap between observational data and structural psychological causes, it allows for a more nuanced and predictive understanding of human behavior. The engine no longer simply records that a coach is "angry"; it understands that the coach is experiencing a "Liberty/Oppression" violation caused by a specific "Institutional Constraint," which has hit their "Norm Compatibility Threshold."

This structural explanation provides the engine with the ability to forecast which topics will produce the "strongest authentic material" before any voice note is recorded. It allows for the creation of content that is not just logically sound, but emotionally resonant and deeply aligned with the subject's core moral modules. As Large Language Models continue to evolve, the integration of Moral Foundations Theory will be essential for creating "morally aware AI systems" that can bridge the gap between human values and automated reasoning.11

The transition from a reactive, observational "Trigger Map" to a proactive, structural "Values Trigger Architecture" ensures that the engine remains a tool for authentic expression. It empowers the coach to speak from their deepest normative architecture, turning the "noise of current events" into a resonant and powerful "voice of truth." By mapping the "moral taste buds" of the individual, the engine ensures that the message always lands with the visceral energy of a core value, rather than the hollow resonance of a generic observation.
