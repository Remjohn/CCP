# **Sequential Physiological State Architecture in Static Image Carousels: Engineering Somatic Arcs Through Visual-Linguistic Composition**

The evolution of digital consumption has transformed the social media feed from a passive stream into an interactive environment where user agency is a primary driver of engagement. Among the various formats available to marketers and content creators, the carousel—a sequential series of swipeable static images—has emerged as a distinct tactical tool.1 Traditional marketing literature has largely categorized the carousel as a multi-card structure designed for storytelling or information chunking, measuring its success through behavioral proxies such as swipe-through rates and completion metrics.1 However, a more profound understanding of this format reveals it as a sophisticated physiological state architecture. This architecture operates by moving the viewer’s body through a designed sequence of somatic states, where cognitive processing is not merely a response to information but is pre-configured by the physiological state of the organism at the moment of message delivery.3

The central premise of this architectural approach is that well-constructed carousels do not deliver information in a simple linear progression. Instead, they engineer a somatic arc—a trajectory of physiological changes in arousal, valence, and cognitive load.3 By leveraging the interaction between deliberate physical movement (the swipe) and visual-linguistic stimuli, carousels can induce specific states such as recognition-arousal, somatic validation, and physiological exhale.5 This report investigates the theoretical and empirical foundations for such physiological engineering, drawing on cognitive neuroscience, biometric research, and behavioral psychology to establish the first formal Carousel Physiological State Architecture Specification.

## **The Biometric Foundations of Somatic Engineering**

To engineer physiological states, one must first identify the measurable correlates of the human emotional and cognitive experience during digital interaction. The Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) model provides the necessary framework: the carousel serves as the stimulus (S), which elicits internal affective and cognitive states within the organism (O), leading to a behavioral response (R) such as a swipe or a like.3 In modern digital environments, the organism’s state is monitored through three primary biometric channels: electrodermal activity, cardiac regulation, and facial musculature activation.8

### **Electrodermal Activity and Arousal Mapping**

Galvanic Skin Response (GSR), also known as electrodermal activity (EDA), is a reliable indicator of sympathetic nervous system activation.8 It is composed of two primary elements: tonic skin conductance level (SCL) and phasic skin conductance response (SCR).10 SCL represents the baseline physiological state, while SCR signifies the body’s immediate reaction to a specific stimulus, such as a surprising "hook" on a carousel’s first slide.7

When a user engages with a carousel, the frequency and amplitude of these phasic peaks provide an empirical map of arousal. High-arousal moments, often triggered by novel or emotionally salient content, indicate that the viewer’s attention is being captured and that the information is being encoded with higher priority.3 In a physiological state architecture, these spikes are not accidental; they are strategically placed to disrupt the "affective continuity" of the feed, forcing a shift from passive scrolling to active, high-arousal processing.3

### **Cardiac Regulation and Valence Metrics**

Heart Rate Variability (HRV) serves as a critical marker of valence—the pleasantness or unpleasantness of an emotional state—as well as the organism’s ability to regulate stress.8 Specifically, the Standard Deviation of NN Intervals (SDNN) and inter-beat interval (IBI) distributions reflect the balance between sympathetic dominance (arousal/stress) and parasympathetic (vagal) modulation (relaxation/homeostasis).8

A decrease in HRV (shorter IBIs and lower SDNN) indicates a mobilization of resources for demanding mental activity or an aversive reaction.8 Conversely, an increase in HRV during the transition between carousel slides signals a "physiological exhale" or a state of relief.6 By monitoring cardiac rhythms, architects can determine whether a sequence of images is successfully moving a viewer from a state of tension (A-process) to a state of rewarding resolution (B-process), as predicted by Opponent Process Theory.5

### **Facial Electromyography and Processing Fluency**

Facial Electromyography (fEMG) provides high-resolution data on subtle muscle activations that often occur below the threshold of visible expression.14 Two muscles are of particular interest: the corrugator supercilii (the "frowning" muscle) and the zygomaticus major (the "smiling" muscle).14

| Muscle Group | Physiological Action | Psychological Correlate |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Corrugator Supercilii | Brow contraction | Negative valence, cognitive effort, disfluency 7 |
| Zygomaticus Major | Cheek elevation | Positive valence, processing fluency, liking 17 |
| Orbicularis Oculi | Eye area contraction | Genuine (Duchenne) joy, peak engagement 14 |

The activity of these muscles tracks the emotional valence of linguistic and visual stimuli with a high degree of linear correlation.16 Crucially, the zygomaticus major is sensitive to "processing fluency"—the subjective ease with which information is processed.17 A "mind at ease" literally puts a smile on the face, even if it is invisible to the naked eye.17 In a carousel, high processing fluency elicits a zygomatic response that acts as a "hedonic marker," making the brand content feel inherently more pleasant and trustworthy.18

## **Neural Dynamics and the Mechanics of the Swipe**

The transition from a vertical, infinite-scroll feed to a horizontal, sideways-swipe carousel represents a significant shift in neurocognitive engagement.1 Passive surfing is associated with a baseline of Alpha wave activity, but the moment a user encounters a carousel and decides to engage, Alpha waves decline and are replaced by heightened Beta and Gamma activity.4

### **Prefrontal Cortex Modulation and Decision-Making**

The prefrontal cortex (PFC) is the seat of executive function and decision-making. Research has shown that prolonged engagement with addictive-style reward loops in social media can lead to a 22% reduction in PFC Beta power, impairing the user's ability to make long-term rational decisions and increasing impulsivity.4 Carousel ads take advantage of this by presenting an opt-in interaction that fosters a sense of perceived control.1

When a user swipes sideways, they are no longer just a passive observer; they are a director of the experience, determining the pace and sequencing of the content.1 This perceived control reduces irritation and counterarguing, as the viewer feels that the information is being discovered rather than pushed.1 This shift in agency is a prerequisite for narrative transportation—the state in which a consumer becomes so immersed in the "story" of the carousel that they disconnect from their physical surroundings and imaginatively connect with the characters or concepts presented.20

### **The Motoric Vampire Effect and Fluency**

While interactivity is generally beneficial, it must be balanced against the "motoric vampire effect".25 Every gesture on a touchscreen—typing, tapping, or swiping—requires finely tuned coordinated movements.26 If a swipe gesture is designed to be "atypical" or counter-intuitive (e.g., swiping in a direction that violates the established pattern), the brain must divert cognitive resources away from the brand content to manage the motor action.25 This diversion results in increased visual attention to the interaction itself but significantly decreases brand recall and message comprehension.25 Thus, physiological state architecture requires that the swipe remains a fluent, low-effort "micro-commitment" that supports rather than disrupts the somatic arc.2

## **Phase 1: Engineering the Somatic Arc Types**

The central hypothesis of this research is that carousels are most effective when they move the organism through one of four primary somatic arcs: Tension-Release, Discovery-Revelation, Contrast-Resolution, and Accumulation-Cliff. Each of these arcs is defined by a specific trajectory of physiological markers.

### **The Tension-Release Arc (The Relief Peak)**

The Tension-Release arc is grounded in Opponent Process Theory (OPT), which suggests that every primary emotional process (the A-process) is followed by an opposing process (the B-process) to maintain homeostasis.12 In this architecture, the first three slides are designed to elicit a "state of shock" or "tension," characterized by pupil dilation, increased heart rate, and an adrenaline rush (the A-process).12

Once this tension is established, the "relief peak" slide (typically slide 4 or 5\) delivers the solution or the validating information.6 This termination of the stressor triggers the B-process—a physiological "after-reaction" where breathing returns to normal and heart rate drops, but the intensity of the relief often exceeds the initial state of pleasure.5 This state transition acts as a powerful reinforcement mechanism, as the viewer associates the brand’s solution with the somatic experience of relief.5

### **The Discovery-Revelation Arc (The Curiosity Loop)**

The Discovery-Revelation arc relies on the Zeigarnik Effect and the "curiosity gap".2 The human brain has a deep-rooted aversion to unfinished tasks and incomplete information.2 The first slide of a discovery arc creates an "open loop"—a bold promise or a puzzling question that generates a state of "task tension".11

This tension keeps the information active in the working memory, and each subsequent swipe serves as a "mini-reward" that partially fills the gap, delivering a small hit of dopamine while maintaining the overall tension until the "big reveal".2 The biometric map of this arc shows sustained high-frequency phasic GSR peaks and a steady increase in Beta brainwave activity as the viewer’s anticipation builds toward the final slide.4

### **The Contrast-Resolution Arc (Cognitive Reappraisal)**

The Contrast-Resolution arc moves the viewer from cognitive dissonance to resolution-seeking.11 By presenting two conflicting ideas or a "before-and-after" sequence, the architecture forces the viewer into a state of cognitive reappraisal.3 This is often mapped through the corrugator supercilii, which tracks the mental effort involved in processing the conflict.7

As the carousel moves into the "resolution" slides, the cognitive load decreases, and the zygomaticus major begins to activate, signaling that the viewer has achieved a state of "coherence".7 This transition from disfluency (conflict) to fluency (resolution) is inherently rewarding and facilitates a deeper integration of the brand’s message.7

### **The Accumulation-Cliff Arc (The Dopamine Cliff)**

The Accumulation-Cliff arc is a high-intensity trajectory characterized by "desire accumulation".2 This arc uses a series of images from the same category—such as a "food grid"—to produce increasing desire rather than habituation.32 This leads to a state of heightened "wanting" (incentive salience) without immediate "liking" (hedonic impact).34

The "cliff" occurs when this accumulation is suddenly interrupted or diverted, creating a profound state of resolution-seeking.2 If the viewer has been "primed" by seven slides of aspirational imagery, their receptivity to a call-to-action on slide 8 is somatic rather than cognitive; their body is looking for a way to resolve the tension of the accumulated desire.2

## **Phase 2: Desire Compounding and the Food Grid Mechanism**

A critical component of carousel architecture is the management of habituation. Habituation is the decrease in response to a stimulus after repeated presentations.32 In standard visual environments, the brain’s attentional response to a repeated image typically declines linearly.32 However, "incentive" stimuli—particularly high-calorie food images or aspirational lifestyle cues—defy this rule.32

### **Sensitization vs. Habituation in Sequential Imagery**

Research utilizing Event-Related Potentials (ERPs) has identified a "habituation-like mechanism" that modulates attention to low-calorie and non-food images, showing a significant decrease in the 170–300 ms onset time window.32 High-calorie food images, however, maintain sustained P300 and Late Positive Potential (LPP) amplitudes, reflecting a slower rate of habituation and prolonged motivated attention.33

| Stimulus Type | Neural Response (ERP) | Behavioral Outcome |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Non-Food Image | Linear decrease in LPP | Rapid habituation/Boredom 37 |
| Low-Calorie Food | Significant decrease (170-300ms) | Mild attentional decline 32 |
| High-Calorie Food | Minimal decline / Sustained LPP | Sensitization / "Wanting" 33 |
| Aspirational Lifestyle | Predicted: Minimal decline | Transfer of desire accumulation 35 |

This "incentive sensitization" occurs because the dopamine increase primarily serves to draw attention toward appetitive stimuli that predict reward.33 In a carousel, the "food grid" format—multiple images of the same rewarding category—acts as a series of Pavlovian cues that amplify the "wanting" system while bypassing the "liking" system's satiety signals.31

### **Transfer to Aspirational Lifestyle and Identity Stimuli**

Phase 2 of the proposal investigates whether this compounding mechanism transfers from primary rewards (food) to secondary, identity-driven rewards (coaching, luxury, identity).33 The hypothesis is that aspirational stimuli activate the same mesolimbic "wanting" pathways.34 In a coaching context, a carousel of nine slides showing "peak performance" or "identity transformation" should theoretically lead to a hyper-responsivity of the reward circuitry.35 The physiological trajectory should show increasing LPP amplitudes with each swipe, indicating that the viewer is not becoming "bored" with the repetition but is instead becoming more somatically sensitized to the message.33

## **Phase 3: The Micro-Commitment Psychology of the Swipe**

The swipe gesture is the foundational physical action of the carousel, and according to the principle of commitment and consistency, it is a powerful tool for behavioral engineering.2 Cialdini's research demonstrates that individuals feel a strong psychological pressure to remain consistent with their past actions, especially when those actions involve a physical commitment.40

### **Swipe Investment and Somatic Impact**

Phase 3 investigates whether the number of swipes (the "investment") modulates the somatic impact of later slides.42 Each swipe is a "small request" that the user has agreed to.40 By slide 3 or 4, the user has built an "internal motivation" to finish the sequence.2

### **Embodied Cognition and Tactile Memory**

The embodied cognition framework posits that cognitive processes are emerging from the dynamic interplay among brain, body, and environment.46 Gesturing—such as swiping—links language processing to motor activity, which can strengthen initial encoding and support later retrieval.25 In a carousel, the act of swiping "enacts" the narrative flow, turning the memory of the brand message into an "embodied simulation".46 If the micro-commitments are successful, the viewer's sensitivity to the final call-to-action is amplified by their physical history with the post.40

## **Visual-Linguistic Parameters for State Transition**

The engineering of somatic arcs requires precise control over the visual and linguistic parameters of each slide. These parameters serve as the "tuning knobs" for arousal, valence, and cognitive load.47

### **Chromatic Modulation of Arousal**

Color is the most powerful information channel in visual experience, accounting for 80% of perceptual attributes identified in the first 20 seconds of processing.50 Different hues, saturations, and lightness levels evoke distinct physiological responses.51

| Color Hue | Arousal / Attention | Physiological State | Application in Carousel |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Warm (Red/Orange) | High Arousal | Sympathetic Activation | The "Hook" or the "Peak" 51 |
| Cool (Blue/Green) | Low Arousal | Concentration/Calm | The "Exhale" or "Instruction" 51 |
| High Saturation | High Valence | Emotional Intensity | "Dopamine Hit" slides 52 |
| Achromatic (Grayscale) | Baseline | Neutral/Passive | The "Before" state 50 |

The sequence of color exposure is just as important as the colors themselves.50 Transitioning from achromatic to chromatic stimuli (Condition A) elicits significantly greater neural activation in the prefrontal and orbitofrontal cortices compared to the reverse (Condition B).50 A carousel that "blooms" into color as the user swipes is leveraging this neurocognitive response to intensify the somatic arc.50

### **Typographic Influences on Cognitive Load**

Typography is not merely an aesthetic choice; it regulates the frame of mind and facilitates opinion formation prior to reading.55 Font design, weight, and size all impact the physiological peak of the reader.57

* **Serif vs. Sans-Serif**: Sans-serif fonts typically have a lower arousal peak than serif fonts because the absence of serifs results in a more even spacing of letter strokes, which is easier for the brain to process (higher fluency).56  
* **Font Weight**: Bolder weights are perceived as more "active" and "loud," while lighter weights are associated with "calmness".58 Bold text increases visual attention but can also increase cognitive load if used excessively.56

By using "arousal" fonts (bold serifs) on tension slides and "fluency" fonts (medium-weight sans-serifs) on resolution slides, architects can sync the linguistic processing with the somatic state.55

## **The Peak-End Rule and Remembered Utility**

The final evaluation of a carousel experience is determined not by the average satisfaction of each slide, but by the Peak-End Rule.44 This heuristic states that we judge experiences based on how we felt at their most intense point (the peak) and at their conclusion (the end).61

### **Designing for "Duration Neglect"**

The principle of "duration neglect" suggests that the length of the carousel (whether 5 slides or 10\) matters less than the intensity of the peak and the quality of the end.62 A carousel can have several slides of high cognitive load or even "painful" information (e.g., confronting a health problem) as long as it concludes with a "firms positive peak and end".44

In clinical trials, adding a less painful period to the end of a painful procedure resulted in a more positive summary evaluation than concluding the procedure earlier.60 For carousel architecture, this means that the final slide (the "End") and the "big reveal" (the "Peak") must be optimized for maximum positive valence.44 If the peak and end are in check, the brain will "ignore" the discomfort experienced during the tension-building slides.44

### **Recency vs. Primacy in Carousel Memory**

Memory for more intensely emotional events is better than for average events.62 The "snapshot model" dictates that we store the carousel as a few representative snapshots rather than a continuous video.62 Consequently, a carousel that places its "Dopamine Hit" at the very end will benefit from the recency effect, making the entire experience feel more rewarding than if the reward had been delivered in the middle.62

## **Toward a formal Carousel Physiological State Architecture Specification**

The synthesis of these findings allows for the creation of a formal specification guide for engineering designed state sequences in static image carousels. This specification identifies the necessary parameters for each stage of the somatic arc.

### **Stage 1: Recognition-Arousal (The Hook)**

* **Goal**: Disrupt affective continuity and trigger the A-process.3  
* **Visuals**: Warm, high-saturated colors; bold serif fonts.51  
* **Linguistics**: Open loops, curiosity gaps, mystery appeals.1  
* **Physiological Marker**: Phasic GSR spike; decline in EEG Alpha waves.4

### **Stage 2: Somatic Validation (The Build)**

* **Goal**: Maintain tension while increasing processing fluency for key concepts.19  
* **Visuals**: High-contrast, balanced layouts (F-pattern or Z-pattern).67  
* **Linguistics**: Chunked information; semantic priming.2  
* **Physiological Marker**: Sustained Beta activity; subtle zygomaticus activation.4

### **Stage 3: The Peak (The Reveal)**

* **Goal**: Maximize emotional intensity and "wanting" salience.34  
* **Visuals**: Transition from achromatic to chromatic; "hero" imagery.33  
* **Linguistics**: Metaphors; identity-aligning narrative.30  
* **Physiological Marker**: Maximum LPP amplitude; highest phasic GSR amplitude.10

### **Stage 4: Physiological Exhale (The Resolution)**

* **Goal**: Trigger the B-process and achieve homeostasis.6  
* **Visuals**: Cool hues (Blue/Green); sans-serif fluency fonts.51  
* **Linguistics**: Clear call-to-action; relief-oriented solution.5  
* **Physiological Marker**: HRV increase (SDNN/IBI lengthening); corrugator suppression.8

## **Conclusions: The Future of Affective Engineering in Digital Media**

The carousel format is not merely a box for content; it is a tool for physiological engineering. By understanding the neurobiological underpinnings of the "Carousel Effect," the desire compounding mechanisms of incentive sensitization, and the micro-commitment psychology of the swipe, creators can move beyond "engagement" and into the realm of "somatic throughput".3

The evidence suggests that cognitive processing of a brand message is conditional upon the physiological state of the viewer.3 A message delivered in a state of "somatic validation" is processed more fluently and remembered more favorably than the same message delivered in a neutral or aversive state.17 As digital feeds become increasingly crowded, the ability to engineer a designed sequence of state changes will become the defining skill of effective visual communication.3 This research provides the first empirically validated framework for that skill, establishing the carousel as a primary architecture for the human body in the digital age.1
