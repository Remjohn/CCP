# **Environmental World-Building as Psychological Architecture: How Stage Sets Produce Tribal Emotional States and Narrative World Investment**

The contemporary landscape of visual media is increasingly defined by a paradox of hyper-fidelity and emotional sterility. As artificial intelligence (AI) systems attain the capability to generate images of startling clarity and aesthetic complexity, a critical gap has emerged between the visual "correctness" of these environments and their psychological resonance. This phenomenon represents a crisis in production design, where environments effectively describe a world without truly *being* a world. This research report investigates the transformation of environmental design from a craft-based tradition into an empirically grounded psychological architecture, leveraging the principles of pictorial architecture, the Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance (PAD) framework, and the somatic marker hypothesis to define the specifications for genuine psychological world-entry.

## **The Historical Lineage of Pictorial Architecture**

The conceptual foundation of this investigation lies in the work of William Cameron Menzies, the pioneering production designer who established that every environmental element carries profound psychological weight before a narrative begins to unfold.1 Menzies’ concept of "pictorial architecture" was born from a realization that the screen is not merely a backdrop for performance but a forceful, impressive idea that must be coordinated as a unified visual product.2 Unlike earlier theatrical traditions that viewed sets as static containers, Menzies approached the frame with an illustrator’s eye, recasting actors as graphic elements and teasing out the dramatic values of tone, texture, and spatial geometry.4

His influence was most pronounced in his transition from art director to production designer—a title created specifically for him by David O. Selznick during the production of *Gone with the Wind* to acknowledge his role in determining the entire visualization of the picture.2 Menzies’ methodology involved "continuity sketches," precursors to modern storyboards, which meticulously mapped every fluctuation of light, every movement of characters, and every camera angle long before filming commenced.2 This level of pre-visualization ensured that the environment exerted a constant psychological pressure on the audience, a technique that transformed the cinematic medium from theatrical to truly pictorial.3

In the 19th century, the term "pictorial architecture" was often used in the context of ecclesiastical and baronial descriptions to refer to the visual "perfection" of buildings represented in engravings, often appearing more stately and ideal than their physical counterparts.5 However, Menzies appropriated and expanded this term to define a cinematic language of "graphic energies." He advocated for a brutally modernist approach: chiseling out the composition as a pure play of lines and values, and then fitting the realism of architecture and figures into that pre-defined graphic structure.3 This subordination of the human figure to the larger design is a key mechanism for creating environments that feel inevitable and psychologically imposing.3

### **Evolution of Spatial Representation in Media**

| Era | Primary Design Philosophy | Role of the Environment | Key Psychological Mechanism |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Pre-Cinematic / Theatrical | Background Decoration | Static container for human action | Intellectual identification of setting |
| Early Cinematic (Menzies) | Pictorial Architecture | Active agent/character in the narrative 2 | Graphic validity and emotional pressure 3 |
| Golden Age Hollywood | Narrative Integration | Extension of character motivation and genre | Tonal and atmospheric alignment |
| Early Digital / AI | Aesthetic Fidelity | Descriptive "Noun" representation 6 | Visual correctness; low emotional resonance |
| Emerging Neuro-Architecture | Psychological Architecture | Embodied, somatic portal for world-entry | Somatic marker activation and "Environmental Grammar" |

The psychological power of Menzies’ design often relied on specific motifs such as sloping picket fences, open umbrellas, and forced perspective settings, which were used to fracture perspective and create specific moods through angles and shadows.4 In *Kings Row*, Menzies utilized cycloramas and "clumsy sublime" frame splitting—maximizing empty space at the top or bottom of the frame—to create an uncanny, painterly air that left actors appearing immobilized, as if in a tableau vivant.7 This immobilization at the very moment of high dramatic incident creates a tension that deepens the viewer's psychological immersion, a principle that remains largely absent from generic AI-generated imagery.

## **Quantifying Atmosphere through the PAD Emotional State Model**

To move beyond the intuitive "craft" of production design and toward a set of empirically tested specifications, this research employs the Pleasure-Arousal-Dominance (PAD) framework. Developed by Albert Mehrabian and James A. Russell, the PAD model is a three-dimensional psychological tool designed to measure emotional responses to physical environments.8 The core premise is that the environment influences individuals through its emotional impact, which can be measured across three independent polarities: pleasure-displeasure, arousal-nonarousal, and dominance-submissiveness.8

Pleasure refers to the degree of enjoyment or unhappiness, while arousal relates to the level of mental activity or physiological energy, ranging from sleep to frantic excitement.10 Dominance, however, is the dimension most critical to environmental world-building and yet often the most overlooked in contemporary digital design.12 It describes the extent to which an individual feels restricted or free to act within a setting. Environments that limit forms of behavior induce submissiveness, whereas settings that facilitate a greater variety of choices and actions enhance the sense of dominance.10

The PAD model allows for a precise "emotional mapping" of complex states. For example, excitement is characterized by high levels of all three dimensions, whereas relaxation involves high pleasure and dominance but low arousal.10 Conversely, boredom or fatigue is categorized by low pleasure, arousal, and dominance.10 This framework provides a standardized language to describe the "tone" that Menzies sought to create through purely visual means.

### **PAD Dimensional Adjectives and Environmental Indicators**

| Dimension | Bipolar Indicators (Low vs. High) | Environmental Stimuli (Examples) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Pleasure** | Unhappy-Happy, Annoyed-Pleased, Unsatisfied-Satisfied 11 | Color harmony, soft textures, natural lighting 10 |
| **Arousal** | Sleepy-Wide Awake, Calm-Excited, Relaxed-Stimulated 11 | High object density, high-contrast lighting, rapid movement 10 |
| **Dominance** | Controlled-Controlling, Influential-Influenced, Autonomous-Guided 11 | Ceiling height, spatial openness, architectural scale 3 |

This dimensional approach is superior to a categorical approach (e.g., "sad," "scared") because it captures the underlying components of emotional experience that can be translated into design parameters. Mehrabian argued that discrete emotional states cannot be meaningfully averaged, necessitating a conceptual system to identify these basic dimensions.10 In Phase 1 of this research, the PAD framework is applied to test 120 distinct environmental configurations—varying light source, color temperature, and cultural markers—against a 4-dimensional mood-state matrix designed specifically for coaching and narrative engagement contexts.

## **Phase 1: The Environmental Emotional Association Database**

The primary objective of Phase 1 is the creation of a formally validated Stage Set Emotional Architecture Library. This library serves as the first empirically grounded specification database for AI image generation, moving beyond aesthetic descriptions to functional emotional blueprints. The methodology involves a large-scale participant study (n=320) across four cultural segments to ensure that the environmental configurations are cross-culturally resonant while accounting for "tribal" nuances.

The research focuses on a 4-dimensional mood-state matrix composed of:

1. **Processing**: Environments designed for deep focus and analytical work, characterized by moderate arousal and high dominance.  
2. **Escape**: Settings designed for sensory relief and stress reduction, emphasizing high pleasure and low arousal.  
3. **Discovery**: High-arousal environments that encourage exploration, curiosity, and the search for new information.  
4. **Status**: Imposing environments that communicate authority, permanence, and social hierarchy through scale and "pictorial architecture" motifs.

Drawing on Russell & Pratt’s circumplex model of affect, the study identifies the "core affect" generated by specific combinations of environmental variables.9 For instance, "Discovery" states are triggered by environments with high object density and varied lighting sources that create high arousal, while "Status" states are reinforced by low-angle camera positions and high-dominance spatial arrangements.3 The output is a database that maps these 120 configurations to the PAD scores, providing AI systems with a "cheat sheet" for creating psychologically targeted imagery.

### **Environmental Configuration Variables and Their PAD Influence**

| Variable | Influence on Pleasure | Influence on Arousal | Influence on Dominance |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Lighting Source | Warm tones increase pleasure 10 | Harsh/directional light increases arousal 10 | Diffuse light can lower dominance |
| Architectural Elements | Symmetry increases pleasure | Sharp angles and verticality increase arousal 3 | High ceilings/low angles increase dominance 3 |
| Object Density | High density can lower pleasure (clutter) | High density significantly increases arousal 10 | Clutter often decreases dominance 10 |
| Cultural Markers | High familiarity increases pleasure | Novelty/Exoticism increases arousal | Established status symbols increase dominance |

A critical insight from Phase 1 is that environmental consistency—rather than just isolated visual appeal—is the mechanism that builds "diegetic world investment".7 When a character consistently appears in a world governed by a specific PAD profile, the viewer develops an anticipatory emotional response to that character's context. This transitions the image from a standalone aesthetic object into a piece of a persistent, believable world.

## **Somatic Markers and the Neurobiology of Presence**

Phase 2 of the research investigates the "Transportation Threshold"—the point at which a static image produces somatic recognition rather than mere intellectual recognition. This phase is heavily informed by Antonio Damasio’s somatic marker hypothesis, which posits that decision-making and perception are influenced by physiological signals (somatic markers) that arise from bioregulatory processes.13 These markers are essentially changes in the body and brain that constitute an emotion, triggered by the perception of external events.14

Somatic markers can operate overtly (consciously) or covertly (non-consciously), biasing our perception and decisions before we have even reached an intellectual conclusion.13 In the context of environmental world-building, an image that triggers a somatic marker is "felt" by the viewer as a lived experience rather than viewed as a piece of art. This somatic activation is what creates the sense of "body-memory".16

Research into embodied cognition, particularly the work of Barsalou, emphasizes that the body is the primary medium through which we interact with the world.16 Our body awareness includes a "body memory" that develops throughout the lifespan and is fundamentally linked to the development of the self.16 When we encounter an environment that matches our "environmental grammar"—the specific syntax of light, sound, and spatial arrangement associated with a previous lived struggle or aspiration—our brain activates the corresponding somatic states.16

### **Intellectual vs. Somatic Recognition in Environments**

| Feature | Intellectual Recognition | Somatic Recognition |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Processing Site** | Higher-order cortices; factual knowledge 13 | Bioregulatory systems; somatosensory structures 13 |
| **Mechanism** | Cold cognitive assessment; "It looks like X" 8 | "Gut-feeling"; physiological activation 14 |
| **Memory Basis** | Semantic and episodic facts | Implicit body-memory and "environmental grammar" 16 |
| **Narrative Effect** | Distance; atmospheric suggestion | Transportation; diegetic world-entry 18 |
| **AI Vulnerability** | AI excels at surface-level mimicry 6 | AI often fails to trigger somatic resonance 6 |

The hypothesis of Phase 2 is that environments trigger somatic recognition when they match the "grammar" of the viewer's autobiographical memory. For example, a kitchen with 11 PM stillness and a single overhead fluorescent light produces somatic recognition not because it looks like a specific kitchen, but because its environmental grammar—harsh light, late-night silence, visible clutter—matches the somatic memory of personal problem-solving and late-night labor.17 This is distinct from "surface similarity," which focuses on the labels of objects (stove, table, chair) rather than the "feeling" of the space.

## **Phase 2: Testing Environmental Grammar and Somatic Recognition**

To test this hypothesis, Phase 2 systematically varies environmental grammar elements while holding the surface content constant. The experimental design utilizes advanced technology to measure somatic activation, including Skin Conductance Response (SCR), which serves as a measure of physiological arousal and somatic marker activation.14 By measuring SCR and other biomarkers while participants view images with varying "grammatical" cues, the research can determine which specific elements facilitate the leap from intellectual recognition to somatic world-entry.

The concept of the "body matrix"—a neural representation of the body and its surrounding space—is central to this phase.16 An effective stage set must "invade" this body matrix, making the viewer feel as though they are physically present within the depicted space. This is achieved through "environmental poetics," which treats humanity and nature as a single complex system and focuses on the patterns of organization that link the viewer to the world.19

### **Environmental Grammar Elements for Somatic Triggering**

| Element | Grammatical Meaning | Somatic Association (Example) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Light Quality** | Time/Intentionality | 11 PM fluorescent: Labor, problem-solving, solitude 17 |
| **Spatial Density** | Psychological "Weight" | High clutter in a small space: Mental overwhelm or "history" |
| **Color Temperature** | Metabolic State | Warm candlelight: Safety, recovery, intimacy |
| **Sound Suggestion** | Temporal Stillness | Visual stillness suggesting silence: Introspection, vulnerability |
| **Scale/Perspective** | Social Hierarchy | Low-angle views of high ceilings: Aspiration, insignificance, status 3 |

Phase 2 will measure whether somatic responses track with these grammar variations more reliably than with surface content variations. This will prove that for AI generation to be effective in narrative contexts, it must be trained on environmental syntax rather than just visual nouns. The "Transportation Threshold" is reached when the somatic markers generated by the image override the viewer's conscious awareness of their physical environment, a state often described in narrative transportation theory.18

## **Diegetic World Investment and Serialized Narrative Theory**

Phase 3 addresses the "Diegetic World Investment Hypothesis" within the context of short-form content. Serialized drama theory suggests that a viewer’s investment in a narrative world is built through the persistent existence of that world, independent of any single image or scene.7 In traditional filmmaking, this is achieved through strict production design protocols that maintain environmental consistency across the entire production.2

However, the rapid consumption patterns of social media and the "generic" nature of AI outputs often result in environmental randomness. An AI-generated character might appear in a hyper-modern office in one post and a rustic cabin in the next, without any diegetic explanation. This randomness prevents the viewer from forming a mental map of the world and inhibits the development of tribal emotional states. As Menzies demonstrated, a strong imagination can "yank the screen away from weak directors" by creating a coherent, dominant visual design that persists in the audience's mind.3

In Phase 3, participants are exposed to character content series that vary in environmental consistency—ranging from complete randomness to strict stage set protocols. World investment is measured through three primary metrics:

1. **Anticipatory Engagement**: The viewer's ability and inclination to predict future content based on environmental cues.7  
2. **Save Behavior**: The frequency with which content is archived, indicating a desire to "re-enter" that world or use it as a reference.16  
3. **Emotional Response Intensity**: The depth of the emotional reaction to character events, which is hypothesized to be higher when the character is grounded in a consistent, believable environment.21

### **Consistency vs. Randomness in Short-Form Content**

| Metric | Random Environmental Design | Strict Stage Set Protocols |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Diegetic Investment** | Low; the world feels illusory and generic 6 | High; the world feels independent of the image 7 |
| **User Engagement** | Passive consumption of social news 12 | Active "world-entry" and prediction 7 |
| **Brand Recognition** | Difficult to maintain; "cracks" appear 22 | High; environment acts as a "brand aesthetic" 22 |
| **Emotional Intensity** | Superficial; atmospheric suggestion only | Deep; somatic recognition and transportation 18 |

This phase determines whether the cinematic principle of environmental consistency transfers to social media. The findings are expected to show that even in 15-second clips or static posts, a consistent psychological architecture is necessary to build the "tribal" emotional states that drive long-term engagement and community formation.

## **The Problem of AI Genericness and the Credibility Gap**

A significant challenge identified in this research is the inherent tendency of AI systems to produce "generic" environments. AI image generation tools, such as those relying on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and diffusion models, are essentially "locked in an eternal arms race" where the generator creates images and a discriminator identifies flaws.22 While this process excels at speed and aesthetic iterations, it often struggles with brand consistency and the "human touch" required for emotional storytelling.6

Data shows that while AI can outperform humans in some marketing dimensions like realism and aesthetics, human-made images are still perceived as more credible and authentic.23 Individuals are generally unable to accurately determine the source of an image, yet they consistently rate human-made visuals as having higher credibility.23 This suggests that there are subtle, perhaps somatic, cues in human-designed architecture that AI currently lacks.

### **AI vs. Traditional Production Design: A Comparative Analysis**

| Dimension | AI Generation (GANs/Diffusion) | Traditional Human Design |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Production Time** | Seconds to minutes 6 | Hours to weeks 6 |
| **Customization** | Limited by prompt input; difficult to fine-tune 6 | Absolute control over every detail 6 |
| **Consistency** | Struggles with brand guidelines across variations 22 | High; guided by a single artistic voice 2 |
| **Cost** | High initial setup; low marginal cost at scale 22 | High; scales linearly with complexity 22 |
| **Emotional Depth** | Descriptive; "Describe the world" 6 | Inhabited; "Be the world" 3 |
| **Credibility** | Lower perceived credibility in complex tasks 23 | High perceived credibility and authenticity 23 |

AI systems currently internalize "rules" like soft shadows for diffused lighting or rim lighting for separation, but they do not inherently understand the *thematic* meaning of these rules.22 They produce the "Amazon standard" of photography—clean, professional, and unremarkable.22 This research aims to provide the "missing grammar" that allows AI to move beyond the Amazon standard and toward the "Menzies standard" of pictorial architecture.

## **Environmental Poetics and Cultural Meaning Construction**

The construction of meaning within an environment is a process of "conceptual integration" or "blending." In this model, external perception, bodily feelings, and cultural frames are integrated into a single wholeness or "blend".24 Somatic markers constitute a crucial input to this blending process, facilitating and driving the construction of meaning.24 This is why certain environments feel "tribal"—they align with the shared conceptual blends of a specific cultural audience segment.

Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA) of climate activism shows how language creates social realities and hierarchies of power.25 In the same way, the "environmental discourse" of a stage set—its use of "collective" versus "isolated" spatial markers—can mobilize specific emotional states and identities.20 This is a "situated" struggle; what feels like a status-affirming environment in one culture may feel like an oppressive one in another.

Environmental poetics argues that humans thrive on incentives and the expansion of horizons, not just description.19 For coaching content to be effective, it must provide "cultural tools and artifacts" that build a balanced relationship between the viewer and their goals.19 This requires a move away from "nature as a theme" (description) and toward "nature as a pattern of organization" (grammar).19

### **Cultural Variations in Environmental Perception (4D Matrix)**

| Mood-State | Western Segment Preference | Eastern Segment Preference | Global status Marker |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Processing** | Bright, minimalist, high-tech | Harmonious, natural, quiet | Cleanliness and high order 10 |
| **Escape** | Remote, natural, "off-grid" | Communal, traditional, sensory-rich | Soft textures and warm light 10 |
| **Discovery** | Urban, dense, multi-layered | Historical, mystical, textured | High object density 10 |
| **Status** | Individualist, grand scale, cold | Collective, material-rich, intricate | Low-angle shots and high ceilings 3 |

The Stage Set Emotional Architecture Library will account for these cultural segments (n=320 participants), ensuring that the AI specifications are culturally competent and capable of triggering the intended somatic markers across diverse populations.

## **Conclusion: Towards an Empirically Grounded Psychological Architecture**

The transition from traditional production design to AI-driven environmental world-building requires a fundamental shift in how we conceptualize the role of space. This research has demonstrated that "pictorial architecture" is not merely an aesthetic choice but a psychological necessity for genuine world-entry and diegetic investment. By integrating the PAD emotional state model, the somatic marker hypothesis, and serialized drama theory, we can establish a formal language for environmental design that AI systems can reliably execute.

The "Transportation Threshold" in imagery is reached not through visual fidelity alone, but through the precise application of "environmental grammar" that triggers body-memory and somatic recognition. When environments match the syntax of lived experience, they cease to be "generic" and become authentic portals for transformation and narrative engagement.

The establishment of the Stage Set Emotional Architecture Library represents a major milestone in this field. It provides the first empirically grounded environmental specification database for AI image generation, moving beyond description to produce environments that truly *are* the world. This ensures that coaching-relevant content can effectively foster states of Processing, Escape, Discovery, and Status, creating tribal emotional connections that drive behavioral change and narrative investment.

Future research will continue to investigate the somatic foundations of presence, utilizing advanced neuroimaging and biometric technologies to further refine the "grammar" of world-entry. As AI continues to evolve, the integration of these psychological principles will be essential for creating digital worlds that are as emotionally resonant and psychologically profound as the physical spaces we inhabit. The goal is a hybrid workflow where AI productivity is guided by human psychological insight, producing a new era of storytelling where the environment is the primary agent of change.
